# Commands

Every action in FElupe Studio is a command. A project is a command list:

```json
{
  "schema_version": 3,
  "commands": [
    {
      "command": "mesh.import",
      "name": "specimen",
      "file": "specimen.vtu",
      "tag": "material"
    },
    {
      "command": "cellset.from_tag",
      "name": "soft",
      "mesh": "specimen",
      "value": 2
    }
  ]
}
```

The JSON schema [`python/felupe_studio/commands.schema.json`](../python/felupe_studio/commands.schema.json) is the single source of truth ([ADR 0003](adr/0003-command-interpretation.md)). The TypeScript types in `app/src/core/commands.ts` are generated from it with `npm run schema:types`, and those of the commands with the values of their expressions in `app/src/core/resolved.ts` (#242); CI checks that they are up to date.

The [command reference](reference.md) lists all properties of the commands; it is generated from the schema by `python docs/make_reference.py`, and a test checks that it is up to date. The [user guide](guide.md) shows how to build a model in the app.

## Load cases

A `loadcase.add` creates the boundaries of a standard test with `fem.dof` on the merged field, created without a displacement (`move=0`). It is referenced like a boundary: the ramp of a step moves its moving end faces, and a job records the reaction force over the displacement of its first moving end face.

| Type       | FElupe                              | Moving end faces (ramped)                           | Options                                    |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| `uniaxial` | `fem.dof.uniaxial(field, axis)`     | `move` at the largest coordinate                    | `clamped`, `symmetry`                      |
| `biaxial`  | `fem.dof.biaxial(field, axes)`      | `move-right-<axis>` of both axes                    | `second`, `clamped`, `symmetry`            |
| `planar`   | `fem.dof.biaxial(field, axes)`      | `move-right-<axis>`; the second axis is held        | `second`, `clamped`, `symmetry`            |
| `shear`    | `fem.dof.shear(field, axes, moves)` | `move` of the face at the largest normal coordinate | `normal`, `compression` (held), `symmetry` |

- `symmetry` (default true, like FElupe) adds symmetry planes at x = 0, y = 0 and z = 0: the model is a quarter or an eighth. Without, the opposite end faces are at the smallest coordinates; a biaxial or planar test then moves them backwards (`move-left-<axis>`, ramped with negative values).
- `clamped` (default false) keeps the end faces rigid in the transversal directions. A uniaxial, biaxial or planar test needs symmetry planes or clamped end faces; the two axes of a test must differ. Like `fem.dof.biaxial`, the edges of clamped faces keep the transversal components.
- A step ramps a list of items (`ramp.items`), each with its own `start` (default 0) and `end`, and the same number of increments (`ramp.num`): `fem.math.linsteps([start, end], num)` per item in the `ramp` of `fem.Step`, one array for items with the same values. An item (`target`) is a boundary (its value, or one value per constrained component of the boundary, #225), a load case (the displacement of its moving faces) or a point load (a factor of its force, #100), at most once per step. FElupe ramps every item with `update(value)`, so further kinds of items (pressures) only extend the kinds that `target` may reference (#90). The `start` and `end` of a boundary with several constrained components are one number per component, in the order x, y, z (a number applies to all of them); the script ramps it with `np.linspace([sx, sz], [ex, ez], num + 1)`, one row per substep. The form takes them as numbers separated by commas, e.g. `0.5, -0.1`, like the `value` of the boundary; the validation checks their count against the constrained components.
- A job evaluates its `steps` in their order, and a step may be repeated (#331): `"steps": ["load", "unload", "load"]` is `fem.Job(steps=[load, unload, load])`, a load cycle without a copy of a step. Every evaluation of a step has its own substeps, counted over all steps of the job; the results name a substep by the step at its position. A name that a list of steps repeats is checked once, e.g. an unknown step.
- Tests: an eighth of a cube of an incompressible Neo-Hookean material gives the analytical nominal stress of uniaxial (λ − λ⁻²), planar (λ − λ⁻³) and equi-biaxial (λ − λ⁻⁵) tension within 0.2 %, and a clamped uniaxial test equals explicit boundaries.

## Symmetry planes

A `symmetry.add {name, axes, x, y, z}` (#186) creates symmetry planes with `fem.dof.symmetry(field[0], axes=(True, False, True), x=0.0, z=0.5)` on the displacement field of the merged field: on the plane `x == x0` the component x is fixed (`skip=(False, True, True)`), the others are free. The planes are those of `axes`, each at its coordinate (default 0, with the unit of lengths); a coordinate of an axis that is not in `axes` is ignored (the form hides it, `x-if-axis` in the schema). Like the boundaries of a load case, its boundaries are entries of `boundaries` named `<symmetry>/symx`, so every step has them. Its kind is `symmetry`: it is in the group of the boundaries of the browser (with its planes, e.g. `x = 0, z = 0.5 mm`), but a step does not ramp it and a job does not take it as a curve.

- The same plane twice prescribes the same degrees of freedom twice with 0, which FElupe accepts. A plane of a symmetry that a load case (`symmetry_planes()`: x, y and z of a uniaxial, biaxial or planar load case with symmetry, the third axis of a shear) or an earlier symmetry already has is a note, not an error (`felupe_studio.commands.notes()`, `symmetryNotes()` in the app, shared validation cases), e.g. *the plane x = 0 of symmetry 'half' is already a symmetry plane of load case 'test'*. Notes do not block Solve.
- The viewport draws the planes like the symmetry of the load cases (a stroke along the normal at every node of a plane, compared with FElupe in `boundaries.fixture.json`) and warns about a plane of the selected symmetry without a node of the drawn meshes.
- Tests: the fixed components equal `fem.dof.symmetry`, and the golden case `bar_symmetry_quarter` (the clamped bar of `bar_loadcase` at y, z ≥ 0.5 with planes at y = 0.5 and z = 0.5) has a quarter of the force of the full bar within 1e-6.

## Material models

The material models are the definitions `Material<Model>` of `material.add` in the command schema, the material registry. Every definition holds the label of the model (`x-label`), how FElupe creates it (`x-felupe`: a `class`, or a `function` of tensortrax for `fem.Hyperelastic`) and its parameters with label, description, bounds and default. The forms, the ribbon, the validation and the generated script read them from there (`felupe_studio.materials` in Python), so a new model needs a schema definition and tests, but no code in the app.

| Model                                    | Label                                | FElupe                                                                    | Parameters                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `LinearElasticSmallStrain`               | Linear elastic (small strain)        | `fem.LinearElastic(E, nu)`                                                | E `E`, ν `nu`                                                            |
| `LinearElasticLargeStrain`               | Linear elastic (large strain)        | `fem.LinearElasticLargeStrain(E, nu)`                                     | E `E`, ν `nu`                                                            |
| `NeoHooke`                               | Neo-Hooke                            | `fem.NeoHooke(mu, bulk)`                                                  | μ `mu`, optional K `bulk`                                                |
| `NeoHookeCompressible`                   | Neo-Hooke (compressible)             | `fem.NeoHookeCompressible(mu, lmbda)`                                     | μ `mu`, λ `lmbda`                                                        |
| `MooneyRivlin`                           | Mooney-Rivlin                        | `fem.Hyperelastic(fem.mooney_rivlin, C10, C01)`                           | C₁₀ `C10`, C₀₁ `C01`, optional K `bulk`                                  |
| `Yeoh`                                   | Yeoh                                 | `fem.Hyperelastic(fem.yeoh, C10, C20, C30)`                               | C₁₀ `C10`, C₂₀ `C20`, C₃₀ `C30`, optional K `bulk`                       |
| `ThirdOrderDeformation`                  | Third-order deformation              | `fem.Hyperelastic(fem.third_order_deformation, ...)`                      | C₁₀ `C10`, C₀₁ `C01`, C₁₁ `C11`, C₂₀ `C20`, C₃₀ `C30`, optional K `bulk` |
| `Ogden`                                  | Ogden                                | `fem.Hyperelastic(fem.ogden, mu, alpha)`                                  | μᵢ `mu`, αᵢ `alpha` (one to three terms), optional K `bulk`              |
| `ArrudaBoyce`                            | Arruda-Boyce                         | `fem.Hyperelastic(fem.arruda_boyce, C1, limit)`                           | C₁ `C1`, λₘ `limit`, optional K `bulk`                                   |
| `ExtendedTube`                           | Extended tube                        | `fem.Hyperelastic(fem.extended_tube, Gc, delta, Ge, beta)`                | Gc `Gc`, δ `delta`, Ge `Ge`, β `beta`, optional K `bulk`                 |
| `LinearElastic`                          | Linear elastic                       | `fem.MaterialStrain(fem.linear_elastic, framework, λ, μ)`                 | E `E`, ν `nu`, `framework`                                               |
| `LinearElasticPlasticIsotropicHardening` | Elastic-plastic, isotropic hardening | `fem.MaterialStrain(fem.linear_elastic_plastic_isotropic_hardening, ...)` | E `E`, ν `nu`, σy `sigma_y`, K `K`, `framework`                          |

A model with an optional bulk modulus is incompressible without it: a section with this material needs the `u-p-J` formulation (`fem.NearlyIncompressible(..., bulk)`), or `u-isochoric` (#241), which takes it as it is, without a volumetric part (the region and the field of `u`, `fem.SolidBody(umat, field)`): its volume changes freely, e.g. of air as a third medium (FElupe ex07). The validation reports `u` with such a material (as before, now naming `u-isochoric`) and `u-isochoric` with a material that has a volumetric part. A bulk modulus marked `"x-felupe": "volumetric"` is added as `fem.Volumetric(bulk=...)`, e.g. `umat &= fem.Volumetric(bulk=5000.0)` for Mooney-Rivlin. The models of `fem.Hyperelastic` are functions of tensortrax: they need the package `tensortrax` in the generated script, which the app loads before the first run of such a model. Their shear moduli are 2 (C₁₀ + C₀₁) for Mooney-Rivlin and third-order deformation, 2 C₁₀ for Yeoh, the sum of the moduli μᵢ for Ogden, Gc + Ge for the extended tube and about C₁ for Arruda-Boyce (exactly C₁ for a large limiting stretch). The lists `mu` and `alpha` of Ogden need the same number of values (`x-same-length` of the parameters, checked by the validation) and no exponent of zero.

**Strain-based materials** (#191, the menu *Strain-based elasticity ▾*, `x-felupe` with a `strain` function and its `statevars`): `fem.MaterialStrain` of a function of FElupe in terms of a strain, not of a strain energy, in a `framework`: `small-strain` (the default: the symmetric part of the displacement gradient and the Cauchy stress), `total-lagrange` (the Green-Lagrange strain and the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress, converted to the first one, for large rotations with small strains), `co-rotational` (the Biot strain U − 1 of the right stretch tensor and the Biot stress) or `seth-hill` with a `strain_exponent` m (the Seth-Hill strain (Uᵐ − 1)/m, e.g. m = 0 for the logarithmic Hencky strain ln U, for large strains), FElupe 12.1 with exact tangents (the package needs `felupe>=12.1.1`). `strain_exponent` is required with `seth-hill` and allowed only with it (`if`/`then`/`else` of the schema; the form shows it with this framework, default 0). Total Lagrange and co-rotational are Seth-Hill with m = 2 and m = 1. For a stretch without rotation, the co-rotational framework gives the forces of small strains (the golden test of the frameworks checks it). The functions take the Lamé constants: the parameters `E` and `nu` (`"x-felupe": "lame"`) are converted by `λ, μ = fem.constitution.lame_converter(E=..., nu=...)` in the line before the material (the names λ and μ cannot be names of objects, which have ASCII letters only); `x-argument` is the name of a parameter of the function, e.g. `σy` of `sigma_y`. Defaults: a steel in MPa (E = 210000, ν = 0.3, σy = 250, K = 1000). A strain-based material has no strain energy for the `u-p-J` formulation, so a section with it needs `u` (validation). The elastic-plastic model is von Mises plasticity with linear isotropic hardening (the return mapping algorithm): the yield stress grows by K times the equivalent plastic strain α, the first of its state variables `(1, (3, 3))` (α and the plastic strain tensor; `fem.MaterialStrain` adds the strain and the stress of the last substep). Plasticity depends on the path: a step whose ramp goes back (`start` and `end` of a ramp item) unloads, and the remaining deformation stays. The results record the state variables of every substep (see Results).

```python
λ, μ = fem.constitution.lame_converter(E=210000.0, nu=0.3)
steel = fem.MaterialStrain(
    fem.linear_elastic_plastic_isotropic_hardening,
    statevars=(1, (3, 3)),
    framework="small-strain",
    λ=λ,
    μ=μ,
    σy=250.0,
    K=1000.0,
)
```

**The Mullins effect** (#346, the pseudo-elastic model of Ogden and Roxburgh): an option `mullins {r, m, beta}` of Neo-Hooke and of the models of tensortrax with a strain energy function of their isochoric part (Mooney-Rivlin, Yeoh, third-order deformation, Ogden, Arruda-Boyce), e.g. `"mullins": {"r": 3, "m": 0.2, "beta": 0.1}`. The material is softer after it was loaded: when it is unloaded and loaded again up to the largest strain energy density ψmax of its load history, and stiff again above it. Its stress is scaled by η = 1 - erf((ψmax - ψ) / (m + β ψmax)) / r, so `r` (> 1) is the reciprocal of the largest softening, `m` (a strain energy density, like a stress) the initial softening modulus and `beta` (≥ 0) its part that grows with ψmax. ψmax is one state variable of every quadrature point; the runner records it after every substep and restores it with the fields of a substep, like the plastic strains (#191), so the stress of a substep is the one of its load history. An option of the material, not a material that wraps another one, keeps the sections, the forms and the references as they are. The extended tube model has no `mullins`, since its parameter `beta` is one of `fem.ogden_roxburgh` too; nor have the compressible Neo-Hooke and the linear-elastic models, whose volumetric part is in their strain energy. The bulk modulus of a model is a `fem.Volumetric` after the softened material, without softening, also of Neo-Hooke:

```python
rubber = fem.OgdenRoxburgh(fem.NeoHooke(mu=1.0), r=3, m=0.2, beta=0.1)
rubber &= fem.Volumetric(bulk=5000.0)

rubber = fem.Hyperelastic(
    functools.partial(fem.ogden_roxburgh, material=fem.mooney_rivlin),
    r=3,
    m=0.2,
    beta=0.1,
    C10=0.4,
    C01=0.1,
    nstatevars=1,
)
rubber &= fem.Volumetric(bulk=5000.0)
```

`fem.OgdenRoxburgh` needs the strain energy function `function` of its material, which `fem.NeoHooke` has and `fem.Hyperelastic` has not, so a model of tensortrax takes the function `fem.ogden_roxburgh` with its own function as `material`. `functools.partial` passes it: `fem.Hyperelastic(fem.ogden_roxburgh, material=...)` would pass the defaults of `fem.ogden_roxburgh`, `mu` of `fem.neo_hooke`, to every function (adtzlr/felupe#1302); the script imports `functools` only then. Both are the same model (the curves of Neo-Hooke agree to 5e-13), with `&` and `fem.Volumetric`, in `fem.SolidBodyNearlyIncompressible` and in `fem.NearlyIncompressible` of mixed fields, also with the model in single precision. The golden cases `cube_mullins_neo_hooke` (u-p-J) and `cube_mullins_mooney_rivlin` (a bulk modulus, `u`) stretch a cube to 1.5, release it, stretch it to 1.5 again, release it, stretch it to 2 and release it, with the steps `[stretch, release, stretch, release, stretch_more, release_more]` (#331); their curves are the homogeneous uniaxial tension of FElupe along the same path (`fem.ViewMaterialIncompressible`).

The ribbon groups the models by `x-group` of their definitions into menus, by the kind of material (#259): *Linear elasticity ▾* (small strain and large strain), *Hyperelasticity ▾* (all hyperelastic models, FElupe classes and functions of tensortrax alike) and *Strain-based elasticity ▾*. The ribbon merges consecutive models of a group, so the definitions of `material.add` are in the order of the menus; a model without a group would be a button of its own. The choice of the model in the properties shows the same groups. The linear elastic model of small strains `LinearElasticSmallStrain` (#259) is `fem.LinearElastic`: the stress of the symmetric part of the displacement gradient, only for small displacements, rotations and strains. Its id differs from `LinearElastic`, the strain-based model, which gives the same curve in the framework `small-strain` (golden test).

## Validation

`felupe_studio.commands.validate(document)` raises a `ValidationError` with all messages, `errors(document)` returns them as a list. Besides the schema, it checks:

- names are unique within the command list, at most 24 characters, no Python keywords and not reserved for the generated script (`field`, `points`, `boundaries`, … and the prefixes `field_`, `solid_`, `region_`, `ramp_`),
- references point to an object of the right kind that was created before (the app keeps the command list in this order, see _Order of the commands_),
- `cellset.from_tag` refers to a mesh imported with a `tag`,
- a material whose model has an optional `bulk` (e.g. `NeoHooke`, `MooneyRivlin`) is only used without `bulk` in a `u-p-J` section,
- a strain-based material (#191) is only used in a `u` section.

The rules are small functions `(model, command) -> problems` in `felupe_studio.rules` and `app/src/core/rules.ts`, with the same names (in camel case in TypeScript, e.g. `ramp_values` and `rampValues`, #310). The `Model` of a command list holds the type of its analysis, the rotation points of its constraints and the objects of the commands before the checked one. Every command is checked by the rules of all commands (`FIRST`: its analysis and its references; `LAST`: one of a kind, a reserved name, a name used before) and by the rules of its command type (`RULES`), and the control points after all commands (`AFTER`: their stiffness). The rules of a 2D model are in `felupe_studio.analysis` and `app/src/core/analysis.ts` (`PLANAR`, one rule per command type). A test checks that both languages have the same rules for the same command types.

## Checks with the mesh data

`felupe_studio.checks.check(document, directory)` reads the mesh files and returns a JSON report (`ok`, `messages`, `cells`, `interfaces`):

- **Cell assignment:** every solid cell of an imported mesh belongs to exactly one section; unassigned cells and cells in more than one section are reported per mesh.
- **Interfaces:** the section meshes are merged on identical points (or with the `decimals` of `mesh.merge`), like `fem.field.merge` in the generated script. For every pair of sections that touch, the report counts the shared points and the _hanging_ points: points on the surface of one section that lie on a face of the other one without being one of its points (tolerance 1e-6 of the model diagonal). Hanging points are not connected, e.g. at a non-conforming interface or if the coordinates of two files differ slightly. In a 2D model, the edges of the 2D cells take the place of the faces.

Example messages: `192 / 192 cells assigned once.` and `Interface left_body / right_body: 25 shared points, 0 hanging.`

## Units

FElupe has no units: the numbers of a model only have to be consistent. The optional `units` of `model.settings` say what they mean, `length` (`mm` or `m`) and `force` (`N` or `kN`); stresses and moduli follow from them (mm and N: MPa, mm and kN: GPa, m and N: Pa, m and kN: kPa), moments are force times length. Nothing is converted: changing the units changes the meaning of the numbers, not the numbers. Rotations of rotation points are always in rad, the ramped values of point loads are factors of their force (no unit), strains have no unit.

`precision` of `model.settings` (ADR 0016, #297) is `double` (the default) or `single`. A model in single precision gets `DTYPE = np.float32` at the top of its script, after the comments of the settings. Its regions are `.astype(DTYPE)` (`REGIONS[…]`, `mixed_region`, `vertex_region` of the control points, `boundary_faces` of the face sets), and its fields have `dtype=DTYPE` (also `mixed_field`, which passes it to `fem.FieldsMixed` or to the field of the displacements). FElupe (≥ 12.1.1) keeps the precision of the regions in `fem.field.merge` and assembles the items in the precision of their fields; its default absolute tolerance of the Newton-Raphson method is an estimate of the round-off of the residual forces. The runner adds the plugin `RoundOff` to the jobs of such a model: the largest absolute tolerance over the largest norm of the reaction forces (on the prescribed degrees of freedom) of the converged iterations, `roundoff` of the result of the job (`null` without reaction forces). The job panel shows it, and warns from 1e-2 on (`ROUNDOFF_WARNING`, `roundoffNote()` in `app/src/core/solvers.ts`). The golden test `test_model_in_single_precision` runs every case in single precision with the runner. The curves agree with double precision within 1e-3 of the largest force (or within the estimate, which bounds the deviation), with an estimate below 1e-2, except the cases of `LARGE_ROUNDOFF`: the sandwich springs and the rotation of a plate, with estimates above 1e-2 and deviations below them. The friction block does not converge. The golden case `bar_two_materials_float32` is `bar_two_materials` in single precision; at its tight tolerance the Newton iterations stop where the round-off of the machine lets them, so its stored curve is compared within 2e-3 of the largest force (two machines, each within 1e-3 of double precision).

The properties of the schema have their physical quantity in `x-quantity` (`length`, `displacement`, `force`, `stress`, `ramped`: the quantity of the ramped target, a displacement, a rotation or a factor, or `parameter`: the `quantity` of the parameter). The app shows the units at the labels of the forms (e.g. `E [MPa]`), in the browser, at the curves, in the tooltips and the control points of the results, and at the scalar bar; the CSV of curves and control points has them in the header (e.g. `u_x [mm]`). The script gets them as a comment.

## 2D models

See [ADR 0010](adr/0010-2d-models.md). `analysis` of `model.settings` (#175) is `3d` (the default), `plane-strain` or `axisymmetric`. A 2D model has 2D cells (`triangle`, `triangle6`, `quad`, `quad8`, `quad9`, of a mesh file or of a mesh script) in the xy-plane; the z of the points of a mesh file is dropped. Plane strain is the plane xy with a unit thickness in z: the reaction forces are per unit thickness. An axisymmetric model has the axis of rotation x and the radius y (y ≥ 0): the reaction forces are the totals over the circumference. The script says so in a comment at its top.

The script makes the fields with `fem.FieldPlaneStrain` or `fem.FieldAxisymmetric` (`dim=2`), mixed fields with `fem.FieldsMixed(..., planestrain=True)` or `axisymmetric=True` (linear quads condensed by `fem.SolidBodyNearlyIncompressible` on the 2D field, #226); linear triangles of a mixed formulation take the MINI element (`RegionTriangleMINI` on a mesh with a midpoint in every cell). `volume_cells(..., dim=2)` reads the 2D cells and rejects 3D cells (and without `dim` the 2D cells of a 3D model) with a message. Boundaries, point loads, symmetry planes and load cases (`fem.dof`) have two components, x and y. The results have three components (z is 0), so the scenes, the extremes and the exports are the same as of 3D models.

The validation (`felupe_studio.commands.analysis_problems` and `app/src/core/analysis.ts`, shared cases) reports, in a 2D model: a 3D `cell_type`, a boundary that constrains z, a point load with a force in z, a symmetry plane normal to z, a load case with an axis in z, a point set by z or a radius about x or y (a radius is about z, a circle in the xy-plane), points, control points or face set centers with z ≠ 0, a face set by z, a constraint that connects z, and a rotation point of an axisymmetric model (a rotation about z is no axisymmetric motion). In a 3D model, a 2D `cell_type` is an issue. The app notes a mesh file whose cells do not fit the analysis. The viewport opens a model in the xy-plane onto the xy-plane (*Iso* returns to it) and draws no boundaries in z; the results have no *Displacement z*, the curve no uz and Fz.

The faces of the 2D cells of a 2D model are their edges: a face set (#175) has the edges of the boundary of a section, on a line `{axis: "x" | "y", value}` or by their centers (`[x, y, 0]`), with the boundary regions of quads (`fem.RegionQuadBoundary`, `RegionQuadraticQuadBoundary`, `RegionBiQuadraticQuadBoundary`, `ensure_3d=True` for the normals in the xy-plane) and a field of the class of the model (`fem.FieldPlaneStrain` or `fem.FieldAxisymmetric`, `dim=2`). A pressure on the edges is per unit thickness (plane strain) or acts on the surface of revolution of the edges (axisymmetric: the force is the pressure times the area 2πr·length). FElupe 12 has no boundary regions of triangles: the app notes it at the face set. In the viewport, the edges of a section are the sides of its cells: the segments of the outline of a cell (the edges of its triangles used once in the cell) from corner to corner (`corners` of the surface of quadratic cells), without sides of two cells of the section; a face set is drawn as a strip along its edges inside the cells, a pressure as arrows normal to the edges, and a click on a cell picks the nearest of its edges on the boundary of the section (the edges at a corner are neighbours of the connected surface). The golden case `cylinder_pressure_axisymmetric` pulls the end of a cylinder of quadratic quads; `tests/test_2d.py` checks the reaction force of a small pressure against the pressure times the area for all quads and both analyses, by a line and by centers.

Control points and multi-point constraints of a 2D model (#175) are in the xy-plane: the control points are a mesh of vertices with a field of the class of the model, a constraint connects x and y (`skip` with two components). The rotation point of a constraint of a plane strain model turns the connected points about z: FElupe takes its first component as the rotation about z and locks the second one. The commands keep the meaning of 3D models: a boundary of a rotation point constrains z (the rotation about z, `skip=(0, 1)` in the script) and a point load at it has a moment about z (`values=[M_z, 0]`); x or y of a rotation point are an issue. The runner and the results show the rotation and the moment of a rotation point (and the curve of its boundary) with three components, z the rotation about z, so the curve is *Moment–rotation* with φz and Mz, like in 3D; the other control points have z = 0. The golden case `plate_rotation_plane_strain` turns the end of the plate by a rigid plate; `tests/golden/test_golden.py` checks its moment against the 3D bar with all displacements in z fixed and its end turned about z.

## Parameters

`parameter.add {name, value, quantity, min, max, step, integer, description}` (#180) is a named number of the model, e.g. a dimension or the number of cells of a mesh made by a script (#182). Its name shares the namespace of all objects and must not be a name of the script. `quantity` (`length`, `force` or `stress`) gives the value its unit of the model settings, in the forms (`Value [mm]`, also of the range), the browser (`50 mm · 10–100`) and the script; `min`, `max` and `step` describe its values, for the open dialog of a parametric project (#183). The validation (`felupe_studio.commands.parameter_problems` and `app/src/core/parameters.ts`, shared cases) reports a value outside of the range, a minimum that is not smaller than the maximum and, of an integer parameter, numbers that are not integers. The browser lists the parameters in the group *Parameters* below *Settings*.

**Derived parameters** ([ADR 0021](adr/0021-derived-parameters.md), #333): the `value` of a parameter may be an expression of other parameters, e.g. `"value": "sheet + layers * (height + sheet)"`, without `min`, `max` and `step`. `felupe_studio.expressions.Parameters` and `evaluateParameters()` of `app/src/core/resolve.ts` evaluate the derived parameters in the order of their dependencies, wherever they stand in the command list (parameters without a dependency between them in the order of the list), before the other fields. Their problems are at the field `value`: a cycle (`'a' depends on itself.`, `'a' depends on itself through 'b' and 'c'.`, at every parameter of the cycle), the problems of an expression, and of an integer derived parameter a value that is not an integer; a parameter or a field that uses a parameter with a problem gets no message of its own (shared cases). The script defines the derived parameters after the free ones, as their expressions with their units, an integer one in `int(...)`:

```python
total_height = sheet + layers * (height + sheet)  # mm
substeps = int(2 * cells)
```

Mesh scripts get them as variables like the free ones. Renaming a parameter renames it in their expressions; deleting a used parameter leaves a problem at them. The open dialog of a parametric project sets the free parameters only, and `set_parameters` of the agent bridge refuses a derived parameter, whose `parameters` give its expression and its value (`computed`). The golden case `sandwich_spring_derived` has the height, the shear and the substeps of the sandwich spring as derived parameters before the parameters that they use, with the curve of `sandwich_spring_expressions`.

## Expressions of parameters

See [ADR 0013](adr/0013-expressions-of-parameters.md). A number of a command may be an expression of the parameters as a string (#242), e.g. `"end": "0.5 * layers * height"`, `"value": "height"` or `"num": "2 * cells"`: numbers (also `1e-3`), the names of the parameters, the constant `pi`, `+ - * /` and `**` (right-associative, before a unary minus on its left), parentheses and the functions `sqrt`, `exp`, `log`, `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `abs` (one argument) and `min`, `max` (two or more), angles in radians. The schema gives such a field the types `["number", "string"]` (`["integer", "string"]`); the range and the step of `parameter.add`, the numbers that name things (the `value` of `cellset.from_tag`, the `ids` of a point set) and the `decimals` of `mesh.merge` are numbers.

`felupe_studio.expressions` (Python) and `app/src/core/expressions.ts` parse an expression by the same recursive descent, without `eval`, and compute with doubles; integer parameters are integers, like in the script. `tests/data/expression_cases.json` (`python tests/data/make_expression_cases.py`) has expressions with their values or messages, and the commands of the golden cases and the samples with the fields that may hold an expression, which both read from the schema (`fields()`, `expressionFields()`). `resolve(document)` (Python) and `resolveCommands(commands)` (app) replace the expressions by their values and report their problems:

- `'h / (n - 3)' is not a finite number.`, also inside an expression, e.g. a division by zero;
- `'layer' is not a parameter in '0.5 * layer * height'.`, `'sinh' is not a function in 'sinh(h)'.`, `'min' takes at least 2 arguments in 'min(h)'.`, `'h *' is not a valid expression.`;
- `'cells / 3' is 1.3333333333333333, not an integer.` of an integer field;
- `'1 - cells' is -3, it must be at least 1.` of a bound of the field in the schema.

The messages name the command and the field, e.g. `commands/21: 'ramp.num' of 'shearing': …`. The validation reports the problems of the expressions first and checks the other rules only without them, with the values (shared cases). The script shows an expression as written, in parentheses unless it is one name or number, with `np.` before the functions of NumPy and `pi` (unless `pi` is a parameter): `fem.math.linsteps([0, (0.5 * layers * height)], num=(2 * cells))`. The values know their expression (a `float` or `int` whose `repr` is the code), so the code generation computes with them like with numbers. Renaming a parameter renames it in the expressions; deleting it leaves an issue. The golden case `sandwich_spring_expressions` is `sandwich_spring_3_layers` with expressions, with the same curve.

In the app, the store keeps the commands as written and gives `resolved`, the commands with the values of their expressions (a command with a problem is left out), for the viewer, the validation, the notes of symmetry planes and the runs; the TypeScript types of the resolved commands (`app/src/core/resolved.ts`, generated from the schema without the strings) have numbers. The forms take expressions (see _Forms (app)_); the browser shows an expression as written. The dialog of the parameters says which objects use a parameter, and lists the problems of the expressions with the typed values before they are applied, e.g. too few increments; they do not keep the values from being applied.

## Meshes made by scripts

`mesh.script {name, file, parameters, item, tag, cell_type}` (#182, ADR 0009) is a mesh made by a Python script next to the project (`file` ends with `.py`). The script runs with the parameters of the model as variables (`parameters`, default all) and makes a variable `mesh`: a `fem.Mesh`, a `fem.MeshContainer`, a `meshio.Mesh` or a dict of names and meshes, of which `item` takes one. `tag` and `cell_type` work as for an imported mesh. The script calls `run_mesh_script(file, {"radius": radius}, item="soft")`, embedded only in scripts with mesh scripts (with `script_mesh()`, which runs the file with `runpy.run_path` and converts the mesh to meshio). The app keeps the script like a mesh file (also in archives; a script named like the FElupe script of an archive stays a script of a mesh) and makes the mesh in the worker with `felupe_studio.meshes.make` by file, item and parameter values, so a changed parameter or a replaced script makes it again. An error of the script is a note at the mesh with its message, and the properties show the traceback of the script (only its frames). Opening a script alone starts a new mesh script with it. `felupe_studio.checks` makes the meshes the same way.

## Meshes made by tools

`mesh.generate {name, tool, parameters}` (#193, ADR 0019) is a mesh made by a tool of FElupe. `tool` selects a definition of the registry of the mesh tools in the schema (the definitions of `MeshGenerate`): its label and group, its FElupe call (`x-felupe`), the dimensions of its points and its cells and its cell type (`x-dimension`) and its parameters with their defaults, bounds and quantities. Part 1 has the primitives `Point` (`fem.mesh.Point`, a vertex with 1D points), `Line` (`fem.mesh.Line`, line cells along x), `Rectangle` (`fem.Rectangle`, quads in the xy-plane) and `Cube` (`fem.Cube`, hexahedrons). The operations (#314) take one mesh made by a tool before (`inputs`): `expand` (`mesh.expand(n, z)`), `revolve` (`mesh.revolve(n, phi, axis)`, the angle in degrees), `embed` (the helper `embed(mesh, value)` of the script: `np.pad` of the points with the coordinate `value`), `translate` (`mesh.translate(move, axis)`), `rotate` (`mesh.rotate(angle_deg, axis, center)`), `mirror` (`mesh.mirror(normal, centerpoint)`, from the `axis` and the coordinate `value` of the plane) and `add_runouts` (`mesh.add_runouts(values, axis, centerpoint, exponent, normalize)`, a value per coordinate perpendicular to the axis); `fill_between` takes two meshes in order (`fem.mesh.fill_between(a, b, n)`: quads between two lines with 2D points, hexahedrons between two quad meshes with 3D points), which have the same cells and points (an input of 1D points needs `embed` first). The form has a select per mesh of a fixed number of inputs, in order. An axis is `x`, `y` or `z` in the commands and its index in the script. The `x-dimension` of an operation is a rule: the cell type of its mesh by the cell type of its input (`{"vertex": "line", "line": "quad", "quad": "hexahedron"}`, or `same`) and the dimensions that its points gain (`expand` keeps the 1D points of a point; `revolve` and `embed` add one). `input_problems()` (Python) and `inputProblems()` (app) report an input of the wrong cell type, points above 3D, an axis that the points do not have, a rotation of 2D points about another axis than z, and a center of the wrong length; an input that is not a made mesh is an error too. The golden case `cube_expanded_from_rectangle` is `cube_yeoh` with its cube a rectangle expanded by 1: the same curve. Their vectors have the length of the dimension of their points (`a` and `b` of a rectangle two coordinates, of a cube three), `n` is the number of points per axis (at least 2), and `b` must be larger than `a` in every axis. The values may be expressions of the parameters. The script has one line per mesh with its name, e.g. `block = fem.Cube(a=[0, 0, 0], b=[length, 1, 1], n=[cells, 3, 3])`, and imports meshio only if the model reads mesh files or runs mesh scripts.

`dimensions()` of `felupe_studio.meshtools` and of `app/src/core/meshtools.ts` give every made mesh the dimension of its points and of its cells and its cell type from the commands alone. A mesh whose points and cells have the dimension of the model is a mesh of the model: it has cell sets (`cellset.from_file`, all of its cells; a made mesh has no cell tags) and so sections. A lower mesh is a construction mesh, e.g. a line in a 3D model or a cube in a 2D one: a cell set of it is an error (*cell set 'hard' takes mesh 'specimen' of line cells (1D), but a cell set of a 3D model needs 3D cells with 3D points.*). `generate()` makes the meshes with the lines of the script and the values of the parameters; `felupe_studio.checks` takes the made meshes of the model like mesh files. The golden case `cube_made_by_tools` is `cube_yeoh` with its cube made by `Cube` and the number of points a parameter: the same curve.

In the app, the worker makes the meshes of the tools (`generate-mesh {commands, name}`, `felupe_studio.meshes.generate()`): the commands of the tools that make a mesh, with the values of their expressions (`generateChain()` of `app/src/core/meshfiles.ts`), so a mesh is made again only when its values change (`generateKey()`, like the meshes of scripts by file, item and parameter values). A mesh of 2D or 3D cells is summarized like a mesh file, a mesh of lines or vertices by its points and cells, with `wire()` of `felupe_studio.scene` for the viewer (its lines as edges, no triangles); the summary has the `dimension` of the mesh. An error of FElupe is a note at the mesh (*Line: ValueError: …*). The viewport draws the meshes of the model like imported meshes, and the construction meshes in gray (`CONSTRUCTION`): lines and light faces (`opacity` 0.35, `edgeColor` of the layer), vertices as markers, with the missing coordinates zero (a line on the x-axis, a rectangle in the xy-plane). They are not part of the point sets on the surface or the previews of point sets. The browser marks them (*Line · n = 7 · construction*) and shows a made mesh below the one mesh that uses it (`usersOf()`); a mesh that another tool uses is hidden by default (`hiddenObjects()`: the objects whose visibility the user changed flip their default), like the history of a CAD model. The worker makes a mesh from its chain (`generateChain()`: the mesh and its inputs, recursively, with the values of their expressions by the schema), so a change of an input makes the meshes that use it again. **Export mesh…** writes a made mesh like an imported one (`export-mesh` with the `commands` of its tools, `export_generated()` of `felupe_studio.meshes`); a mesh of lines or points has no solid cells to export.

## Generated script

`felupe_studio.codegen(document)` validates the command list and returns a FElupe script that only needs FElupe, NumPy and meshio (and tensortrax for its models). Its structure does not depend on the order of the commands:

0. the parameters, as variables with their units as comments (after the comment of the units, before the helpers of the script)
1. meshes (`volume_cells()` keeps the solid cells of a meshio file, of one cell type, and ignores lower-dimensional blocks) and cell sets
2. materials
3. a region and a field container per section, merged with `fem.field.merge([...])` into the top-level `field`. The region follows the cell type (`REGIONS`); for `u-p-J`, `mixed_region()` gives linear tetrahedra a bubble point (`tetra5`, `fem.RegionTetraMINI`), the other cell types keep their region. `mixed_field()` makes the displacements only of linear hexahedrons and quads, whose constant pressure and volume ratio (Q1/P0) `fem.SolidBodyNearlyIncompressible` condenses in every cell (#226), and `fem.FieldsMixed` of the other cell types (Q2/P1, P2/P1, MINI); `nearly_incompressible()` makes the solid body that fits the field. The helpers are in the script only with a `u-p-J` section. A test runs every cell type in both formulations, and one compares the condensed with the mixed fields (3D, plane strain, axisymmetric).
4. solid bodies, created after the merge (required for items with an internal state)
5. point sets on `field.mesh_container.points`, face sets (boundary regions of the merged meshes of the sections, with a field linked to `field`), load cases of `fem.dof` on `field` and `boundaries` on `field[0]`; the boundaries of a load case are added as `<load case>/<key>`
6. steps and jobs, evaluated with `job.evaluate(x0=field, tol=0.01)` (skip with `evaluate=False`)

Generated scripts pass `ruff check` and `ruff format --check`. The tests run them with FElupe and compare with hand-written FElupe models.

The code generation follows ADR 0018: the script is a fixed sequence of parts (`PARTS` of `felupe_studio.codegen`), each a function of a `Context` that returns its lines. The context is computed once from the resolved commands: the type of the analysis, the precision, the units and the sets that several parts use, e.g. the load cases or the rotation points of the constraints. Within a part, the lines of an object are made by the emitter of its command type, a function `(context, command) -> lines` of a registry of the part, in the order of the commands. The parts and their emitters are in `sections.py`, `sets.py`, `items.py` and `jobs.py`, the Python source formatted like ruff in `formatting.py`. A new command type is a new emitter in the registry of its part; a test checks that every command type has one.

## Model store (app)

`app/src/core/store.ts` holds the command list in the browser:

- `apply(edit)` with `add` (optionally at an index), `update` (also renames) and `remove`; an array of edits is one undo step and is applied completely or not at all
- `undo()`, `redo()` with up to 100 steps; `load(document)` replaces the model and clears the history
- derived state: `issues` (validation, `app/src/core/validate.ts`), `tree` (browser tree, `app/src/core/tree.ts`), `resolved` (the commands with the values of the expressions of the parameters, #242) and `dependents(name)`; `setReport(report)` adds the check report of the worker until the commands change
- `subscribe(listener)` for the UI

The TypeScript validation mirrors the Python validation. Both run the shared cases in `tests/data/validation_cases.json`, so their messages are identical; after changing a message, run `python tests/data/make_validation_cases.py`. Reserved names are part of the schema (`x-reserved`).

## Project files

A project is saved as `<name>.felupe.json`, the command list with a stable layout so that it diffs well:

- `schema_version` first, then `commands`; every command is one block that starts with `command` and `name`, the other keys follow in the order of the schema (unknown keys sorted at the end),
- two spaces of indentation and a newline at the end,
- numbers as written by JavaScript's `JSON.stringify` (e.g. `1e-8`, `0.0000025`).

The app (`app/src/core/project.ts`: `serialize`, `parse`) and Python (`felupe_studio.project`: `dumps`, `loads`) write byte-identical files; both are tested with `tests/data/*.felupe.json`. Loading checks the JSON schema (Ajv in the app, jsonschema in Python) but not the references between commands, so a file with a broken reference still opens and shows its issues in the browser.

Older files are brought to the current schema with `MIGRATIONS[version]`, one step per schema version; a file of a newer schema version is rejected with a hint to update FElupe Studio.

| From | To  | Migration                                                                                                                                         |
| ---- | --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | 2   | The ramp of a step gets items with their own values: `{boundary, also, start, end, num}` becomes `{num, items: [{target, start, end}, …]}` (#90). |
| 2    | 3   | `"cutback": true` of a job becomes `"cutback": {}` (the settings of the cutback, #133), `false` is removed; `model.settings` is new (#132).       |

## Forms (app)

The form of the selected object is shown in the **properties panel** on the right of the viewport (#83). It opens when an object is selected (in the browser, in the history bar, with `select <name>` and after an object was created) and closes with ×; the selection stays, and a click on the object opens the panel again. The panel belongs to the Model workspace; on narrow screens it is shown below the browser.

The forms of the app are not written per command. `app/src/core/form.ts` derives them from the schema:

| Schema                                                                    | Field                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `name`                                                                    | name field, checked for the pattern, reserved names and duplicates                                       |
| reference to a `Name` (single or array)                                   | selection of all objects of that kind (except the edited one), also of the ones created later; see below |
| `string`, `number`, `integer`                                             | text and number fields with the ranges of the schema; with `"string"` also an expression (#242)          |
| `enum`, array of an `enum`                                                | selection, checkboxes                                                                                    |
| `oneOf` of definitions with a constant (`model`) or of objects (`select`) | variant selection that switches the fields below                                                         |
| optional object (`ramp`)                                                  | checkbox that switches a group of fields on and off                                                      |
| array of objects (`ramp.items`)                                           | rows with a field per property, add and remove; a `unique` column offers an object in one row only       |
| `if` / `then` / `else` (`bulk` of a section)                              | field that is only shown and required for a condition                                                    |

`app/src/core/registry.ts` adds what the schema does not know: labels, hints, placeholders, defaults of new objects, the ribbon tab of a command and the prefix of suggested names (`material_1`). A test checks that every field of every variant has a label (from the registry or, for the material registry, from the schema), and that the form validation rejects what the schema rejects. An `initial` function adapts the defaults of a new object to the model: a new section takes the first cell set without a section, and `u-p-J` if its material is incompressible (no bulk modulus).

The ribbon (`app/src/core/ribbon.ts`) shows one button per command of a tab (the `tab` of the registry), or one button per variant if the variant selects a schema definition, e.g. one button per material model. A button opens a dialog with the form of a new object: a suggested name, the defaults of the registry and the latest object of the right kind for references. A command whose required references do not exist yet is disabled with the reason ("Create a cell set and a material first."); commands marked `single` (the merge setting) are disabled once the model has one.

Changes are applied with the model store: `store.update(name, command)` is one undo step, and if an object is renamed, the references of the other commands follow.

A number field whose schema allows a string (`["number", "string"]`) takes an expression of the parameters (#242): the text is a number if it is one, else the expression as written, without a leading `=`. A list of numbers takes expressions separated by commas outside of parentheses, e.g. `0, min(h, 2), 2 * h`; numbers may still be separated by spaces. The form checks an expression with the values of the parameters of the model, with the messages of the validation, and its value against the bounds of the field, e.g. *'1 - cells' is -3. Must be at least 1.*; below the field (or below the row of a ramp) it shows the value with its unit, e.g. *= 12 mm*. In a model with parameters these fields have a text keyboard on touch screens; without parameters a numeric one. An issue of a stored expression is shown at its field, also in a row.

### Adding a material model

1. Schema: a definition `Material<Model>` with `model` as a constant, `x-label`, optionally `x-group` (a menu of the ribbon), `x-felupe` (`class` or tensortrax `function`) and its `parameters` with `x-label`, `x-quantity` (the unit), `description`, bounds and `default`, added to `MaterialAdd.oneOf` next to the models of its group (`python/felupe_studio/commands.schema.json`); run `npm run schema:types`.
2. Tests: the registry tests check the signature of FElupe and a stress-free state with a positive shear stiffness; add a golden case `cube_<model>` (uniaxial tension of a cube with `tol: 1.49e-8`, compared with `fem.ViewMaterialIncompressible`) or a code generation test that runs the model with FElupe.

The form, the ribbon button and the code generation follow without further code (see [Material models](#material-models)).

## Command line (app)

The command line below the viewport accepts the same JSON as the project file (`app/src/core/commandline.ts`):

| Input                                                                           | Action                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `material.add {"name": "soft", "model": "NeoHooke", "parameters": {"mu": 1}}`   | create an object; a missing name is suggested, missing values get the defaults of the registry |
| `{"command": "material.add", ...}`                                              | the same, as one JSON object                                                                   |
| `section.add`                                                                   | open the dialog of a new section                                                               |
| `delete <name>`, `select <name>`                                                | delete (if unused) or select an object                                                         |
| `undo`, `redo`, `help`                                                          |                                                                                                |

A created command is checked like a form (`draftErrors`), keys that are not part of the command are reported instead of being dropped, and finally the command is validated against its own schema definition (`commandErrors` in `project.ts`, Ajv). On an error the line stays for correction.

Tab or Enter accepts a suggestion: command names and keywords for the first word, object names after `delete` and `select`, and after a command a JSON template with the defaults (one per material model). ↑/↓ move through the suggestions, or through the entered lines if no suggestions are shown; Escape closes the suggestions, a second Escape clears the line.

**Order of the commands:** a form offers every object of the right kind, also one that was created later (#80). After every edit, the store puts the command list into an order in which every reference points to an object before (`order()` in `app/src/core/validate.ts`, a stable topological order): a command that references a later object moves behind it, together with the commands that depend on it, and all other commands keep their order. A command without references (e.g. `mesh.import`, `material.add`) never moves. The reordering is part of the edit, so one undo restores it; the saved project file and the generated script are always in order.

The history bar below shows the command list in order: a click selects the object, objects with issues are marked, and the arrows step back and forward (undo, redo).

## Mesh files (app)

A project file references mesh files by name; their content lives next to it. In the app:

- **Import mesh** (ribbon, `mesh.import` on the command line, or opening/dropping a single mesh file) opens a dialog: choose or drop the file, see its cells, points, bounds and ignored lower-dimensional blocks, and create the mesh together with its cell sets in one undo step: one cell set per value of a cell tag (named after Gmsh physical groups if the file has them), one with all cells, or none.
- **Files with more than one type of solid cells** (e.g. hexahedra and tetrahedra) give one mesh per cell type, like the meshes of the `MeshContainer` of `fem.mesh.read()`: a region of FElupe has one cell type. The dialog names them `<name>_<cell type>` and sets their `cell_type`; a tag value in cells of more than one type gives one cell set per type. The meshes share the points of the file, so `fem.field.merge()` connects them without `mesh.merge`. Wedges and pyramids have no region in FElupe; they are listed as not imported. A direct transition from hexahedra to tetrahedra shares the corner points, but a quad face meets two triangles: the displacements are not conforming inside these faces.
- **Open** accepts a project file together with its mesh files (select several files, or drop them on the window), or a project archive (#149, `.felupe.zip`, or any `.zip` or ZIP file by its first bytes): the archive is unpacked into the same list of files (with `app/src/core/archive.ts`, loaded on demand), so one path opens files, archives and samples. Several mesh files without a project, also of an archive without a project file, are offered in the import dialog. Opening a project replaces the mesh files of the previous one, so a project that references a file of the same name does not use the mesh of the previous project; with unsaved changes the app asks first (the samples dialog warns itself). Files of an archive that the project does not use are listed in a note; an archive that cannot be opened (not a ZIP file, two project files, unsafe paths) keeps the current model. The header shows the name of the archive (`<stem>.felupe.zip`, also for a `.zip`).
- **Format of a project** (#150): the suffix of its name, `.felupe.json` (project file) or `.felupe.zip` (archive). A project opened from an archive or a sample is an archive, one opened from a project file stays a project file; a new project is a project file until its first `mesh.import`, then an archive. **Save project** saves in that format without asking: an archive with the mesh files that are loaded (a note lists the missing ones). **Save project as…** (`app/src/ui/SaveAsDialog.tsx`) chooses the name and the format, for an archive also the FElupe script (`<name>.py`, from `client.script()` like the export, so only once Python is ready) and shows the mesh files with their sizes and the total; a mesh file that is not loaded is listed as missing, saving is still possible. The name and with it the format are kept in the session. **Rename** (#205): the name in the header is a button (*Rename the project*) that turns into a field with the stem and the suffix beside it (also *File ▸ Rename project…*, also in narrow windows where the name is hidden); Enter or leaving the field renames, Escape cancels, and an invalid name (`projectNameProblem()` in `app/src/core/files.ts`: empty, one of `\ / : * ? " < > |` or a control character, a leading dot) is shown below the field and not taken. The rename changes only `fileName`: the next save downloads under it, the exports and the parameters dialog take it, the session keeps it. A renamed project is not saved until it is saved under its name (the app compares the name with the name of the last saved or opened file; the change of a new project to an archive is no rename). *Save project as…* refuses a name whose script `<name>.py` would be a mesh script of the project (#182) when the archive includes the script, since the archive would have two files of that name.
- A mesh whose file is not loaded, cannot be read, lacks its cell tag or needs a `cell_type` (or names one the file does not have) is marked in the browser; its properties show the file state with **Choose file…**, which stores the chosen file under the name the mesh references.

**Project archives** ([ADR 0007](adr/0007-project-archives.md), #148, #150): `<name>.felupe.zip` is a plain ZIP file with the project file `<name>.felupe.json` and the mesh files under the names of their `mesh.import` at its root, optionally with the FElupe script `<name>.py`, so the unpacked folder runs with `python <name>.py`. `app/src/core/archive.ts` writes the same bytes for the same project (fixed times, sorted mesh files) and reads archives that users zipped themselves (one top-level folder is stripped; folders, hidden files and `__MACOSX/` are skipped). Absolute paths, `..` and two project files are errors; files that the project does not use are reported; an archive without a project file gives mesh files to import.

The files are kept in the browser (`app/src/core/meshfiles.ts`) and written into the working directory of Python in the worker, where `felupe_studio.meshes.load()` reads them with meshio exactly like the generated script (`volume_cells`): a summary for the dialog (`inspect()`, one entry per type of solid cells) and the outer surfaces for the viewport, from one read. meshio is served as a pinned wheel next to FElupe (`config.wheels` in `app/package.json`). They are kept across a reload of the page, see _Session in the browser_.

## Viewport (app)

The viewport draws the imported meshes with the WebGL2 viewer of FElupe ([ADR 0004](adr/0004-viewer.md)), a pinned copy in `app/src/vendor/felupe-viewer/`.

- `felupe_studio.scene.surface()` triangulates the outer surface of the solid cells with FElupe (`triangulate_surface`), like `mesh.plot()`: flat faces for linear cells, subdivided curved faces for quadratic cells, and the edges of the faces. Every region of cells with the same values of all cell tags is triangulated on its own, so its surface is closed also at the interfaces to other regions (cell sets and sections take the cells of a tag value or all cells). The arrays, with the values of the cell tags per cell, go to the main thread as little-endian bytes (`float32` coordinates, `uint32` indices, `int32` tags) and are drawn without copies.
- **Color by** (toolbar) colors the cells by mesh, cell set or section. By default, the viewport takes sections if the model has any, else cell sets, else meshes, until another one is chosen. Colors follow the position of an object in the model, so hiding an object does not repaint the others. The legend names the cell sets or sections with visible cells, and _No cell set_ or _No section_ for the other cells. Later cell sets win for cells in more than one. A ninth and further object is gray instead of a generated color. A mesh may have an own `color` (`#rrggbb`, #336) of `mesh.import`, `mesh.script` and `mesh.generate`, which *Color by: Meshes* and its legend take instead of the color of its position (`automaticMeshColor()` of `app/src/viewer/layers.ts`); the other meshes keep theirs, and construction meshes stay gray. It is a property of the model, not of the script: the generated script is the same.
- **Visibility:** the eye of a mesh, a cell set or a section in the browser hides its cells (and their edges). The surfaces of the remaining regions stay closed.
- **Point sets** are shown as markers on the nodes of the drawn meshes that they select, with the tolerances of `np.isclose` in the generated script (`atol` and the default `rtol = 1e-5`); the selected point set is highlighted, its eye hides it. Point sets of point ids refer to the merged mesh of the script and are not shown.
- **Point sets at a bound and on a cylinder** (#181): `select: {bound: "min" | "max", axis, atol}` selects the points at the smallest or largest coordinate of the meshes of the sections, without the control points: the script collects the points of the cells of the sections (`solid_points`, from the merged regions of the sections) and takes `np.isclose(points[:, 2], points[solid_points, 2].max(), atol=1e-8)`. `select: {radius, axis, center, atol}` selects the points at the distance `radius` from the line through `center` (default the origin) parallel to the axis, `np.isclose(np.hypot(points[:, 1] - 5, points[:, 2] + 3), 25, atol=1e-8)` for an axis x. Both have the default `rtol` of `np.isclose` (1e-5), like a plane. In the viewport, the bounds are those of the drawn meshes; a point set by a plane, at a bound or on a cylinder that selects no node is a warning when it is selected. A picked plane at a bound of the drawn meshes creates a point set at that bound (`z == max`), other planes keep their coordinate. In the form, the variants are *By coordinate*, *At a bound*, *On a cylinder*, *On the outer surface*, *By nodes* and *By point ids*; a chosen variant gets the defaults of its required fields.
- **Point sets of a section** (#241): `select: {section}` selects all points of the cells of a section, `np.isin(np.arange(len(points)), region_<section>.mesh.cells)` (the merged points; of the MINI element with its bubble points), e.g. of a rigid part whose points are all moved by boundaries: a section of a stiff material whose points are all prescribed has no strains for a translation, so its stiffness does not matter (the engine mount of ex07 has its metal parts without a solid body; the sample has them as sections of steel). The reference `select.section` is a path of fields (`REFERENCES` of `commands.py` and `validate.ts` take paths joined by dots), checked, renamed and listed like other references. The viewport previews the nodes of the cells of the section on the surface of its mesh (of a 2D model all of them).
- **Point sets on the outer surface** (#222): `select: {surface: true}` selects the points on the outer surface of the meshes of the sections, e.g. the points of a contact: `surface_points([region_a.mesh, region_b.mesh], points)` takes the faces which belong to one cell of all sections (`triangulate_surface` of FElupe, like `mesh.plot()`, with the merged points, so a face between two sections is inside; the corners of the MINI element without the bubble points), of 2D cells the edges which belong to one cell (corners and mid points). The helper is in the script only with such a point set. The viewport previews the nodes of the surfaces of the drawn meshes (of a 2D model the nodes of their outline); every mesh and region of tags has its own closed surface there.
- **Control points and reference points on point sets** (#222): a point set by a plane, by nodes or on a cylinder also selects the vertices of control points and reference points of rigid surfaces at its coordinates (`np.isclose` of all merged points), so its boundaries move them. The validation (shared cases) counts these boundaries for the stiffness of the vertex, e.g. of a rigid plane whose reference point is on the plane of a bonded face (sample *Hyperelastic spring with end stops*). Point sets at a bound, on the surface and the points of contacts take the points of the cells only.
- **Point sets by nodes** (`select: {points: [[x, y, z], ...], atol}`, #92) store picked nodes by their coordinates, not by ids of the merged mesh, which change when sections are added or removed. The script selects them with `points_at(points, coordinates, atol=1e-8)` (a helper of the script, `np.isclose` of all three coordinates) and raises a `ValueError` for a coordinate without a point. In the properties, the coordinates are typed one point per line (`x, y, z`); the viewport warns about the coordinates of the selected point set without a node of the drawn meshes.
- **Face sets** (`faceset.add {name, section, select}`, #142, [ADR 0008](adr/0008-face-sets.md)) are faces of the boundary of a section: on a plane (`{axis, value, atol}`, the faces whose points are all on it) or by their centers (`{centers, atol}`, the mean of the points of a face; the nearest face within `atol`, by default 1e-6 of the size of the section). The script makes a boundary region on the merged mesh of the section with `boundary_faces()` (`fem.RegionHexahedronBoundary` and the quadratic variants, `BOUNDARY_REGIONS`; embedded only with face sets) and a field of it, linked to the merged field (`field_<name>.link(field)`). Faces of tetrahedrons have no boundary region in FElupe 12: the app notes it at the face set (with the mesh file read) and blocks Solve. In the viewport (`app/src/viewer/facesets.ts`), the faces of a section are the triangles of its cells grouped by the face of their cell (`faces`, or `curved` of quadratic cells), without faces of two cells of the section; face sets are light faces, the selected one highlighted, and the viewport warns about centers without a face. A picked face offers **Create face set** of the faces of its section on its plane (named e.g. `faces_z_max`); Shift+click picks faces of one section (another section starts over, a picked face again removes it), and **Create face set** stores their centers (named `faces`).
- **Control points** (`point.add {name, coordinates}`, #94) are points that are not nodes of the meshes, e.g. the center point of a multi-point constraint. All control points form one mesh of vertices whose field is merged after the fields of the sections (`region_control_points = vertex_region([...])`); a control point is a point set of one point, found by its coordinates, so boundaries and the curve of a job reference it like a point set. It has no stiffness of its own: every component needs a boundary or a multi-point constraint (as its center point, or all components as its rotation point). The results and exports show the cells of the sections only. See [ADR 0006](adr/0006-control-points.md).
- **Rigid surfaces** (`rigid.add {name, coordinates, shape}`, #219, see [ADR 0011](adr/0011-rigid-surfaces-and-contacts.md)) are a reference point with a shape: a plane `{normal}` through the point (the outward normal, towards the points that touch it) or a cylinder `{radius, axis, inside}` whose axis goes through it (`inside` for a tube around the points, else a roller). The reference point is a vertex of the mesh of the control points and a point set of one point named like the surface, so boundaries move the surface (it does not rotate; FElupe keeps its normal and its axis), a point load acts on it and the curve of a job may be a boundary of it; the results show it like a control point. The script lists the shapes as comments with the control points; contacts (`fem.ContactRigidPlane`, `fem.ContactRigidCylinder`) will connect points to them. The validation (shared cases) reports a normal or an axis of zero length, a reference point at the coordinates of a control point or of another reference point, components of the reference point without a boundary (no stiffness), and in a 2D model a z coordinate, a normal with a z component or an axis other than z (a plane of a 2D model is a line, a cylinder a circle). The viewport draws a plane as a translucent rectangle around the model (the projection of its center onto the plane, 1.2 times its size) with the normal as an arrow at the reference point, and a cylinder as a translucent tube along the model; in a 2D model a line is a strip on its rigid side and a circle a disc (a roller) or a ring (a tube). In the results, the shapes move with the displacements of their reference points (`app/src/viewer/rigid.ts`).
- **Multi-point constraints** (`mpc.add {name, points, center, constrained, multiplier, rotation}`, #97) connect the points of a point set to a center point: `fem.MultiPointConstraint(field, points=np.flatnonzero(points), centerpoint=single_point(center, "center"), ...)`. The center point and the optional rotation point are control points (or point sets of one point; `single_point` raises a `ValueError` for another number of points). `constrained` are the connected components (`skip` for the others); with a rotation point, whose values are the rotations about the center point in rad, all components are connected. The constraints are items of the steps (`constraints`, default: all). The validation (shared cases) reports a rotation point with skipped components, control points at the same coordinates (the script merges them; place a rotation point beside the center point) and components of a control point without a boundary or a constraint (no stiffness).
- **Contacts** (`contact.add {name, points, surface, friction, multiplier, elastic_slip}`, #220) connect the points of a point set, or of a face set, to a rigid surface (#219): `fem.ContactRigidPlane(field, points, centerpoint, normal, ...)` of a plane, `fem.ContactRigidCylinder(field, points, centerpoint, radius, axis, inside, ...)` of a cylinder, with the reference point of the surface as the center point. The points of a point set are those of the cells of the sections (`np.intersect1d(np.flatnonzero(points), solid_points)`: a point set by a plane would also take the vertices of control points on it), of a face set the points of its faces (`np.unique(region_<faces>.mesh.cells_faces)`). `friction` is the Coulomb coefficient (default 0), `multiplier` the factor of the penalty (default 1) and `elastic_slip` the allowable slip of sticking points (a length, default none). `items` are the solid bodies of all sections, so the penalties are factors of the mean stiffness at the points (FElupe's recommendation); the script assembles the stiffness of the solid bodies once before the contacts, because FElupe would assemble them with the field of the contact, which fails with the vertices of the control points. Contacts are items of the steps, with the multi-point constraints (`constraints`, default: all); the force of a contact acts on the reference point, so the curve of a job on a boundary of the surface is the contact force over its displacement, and the results of the reference point (card *Control points*, CSV) have it. The validation (shared cases) reports a surface that is not a rigid surface and a contact of a control point. In a 2D model (#221), a plane is a line with a normal in the xy-plane (`normal=[nx, ny]`) and a cylinder a circle about z (`fem.ContactRigidCylinder` without an axis); in an axisymmetric model a line normal to x is a plate on the axis, a line normal to y or a circle is a surface of revolution (a tube or a torus). The penalty of a point does not depend on its radius, so the contact pressure of an axisymmetric model is exact only in the limit of a large multiplier: the golden cases `plate_contact_plane_strain` and `cylinder_contact_axisymmetric` (pressed at their end by a line, multiplier 1000) agree with a prescribed displacement of the end within 1e-3, and `plate_contact_roller` presses a circle onto a plate in plane strain. The golden cases `block_contact_plane` and `block_contact_friction` press a block by a plane without and with friction; their tests compare the force with a prescribed displacement of the top (free or fixed in x and y) within 1e-3, the penetration of the penalty. `block_contact_cylinder` presses a roller onto the block; its test checks that the points in contact are on the cylinder. In the viewport, the selected contact highlights its points and its surface.
- **Boundaries and curves of control points** (#98): a boundary of a center point moves the connected points rigidly (its components and value, ramped by a step); a boundary of a rotation point prescribes rotations about the center point in rad. The curve of a job on a boundary of a control point shows its reaction force over its displacement, on a rotation point the moment over the rotation (_Moment–rotation_, `φ` and `M`, also in the CSV: `phi_x`, `M_x`, …); the moment equals `results.moment` of the constraint. The properties of a rotation point and of its boundaries say that their values are rotations.
- **Point loads** (`load.add {name, points, force}`, #100) apply the force at every point of a point set or at a control point: `fem.PointLoad(field, points=..., values=[fx, fy, fz])`, an item of the steps (`loads`, default: all). A load is applied in full unless a step ramps it: a ramp item of a load is a factor of its force (`np.outer(ramp, force)`), and a load keeps its last value in the following steps. The curve of a job on a load is controlled by the force: `fem.CharacteristicCurve(boundary=load, items=[solids, constraints])` records the displacement of the first point of the load over the total force (the sum of the internal forces at its points). In the viewport, a load is drawn as arrows in the direction of its force.
- **Pressures** (`pressure.add {name, faces, pressure}`, #105) act on the faces of a face set (#142): `fem.SolidBodyPressure(field_<faces>, pressure=p)`, a force per deformed area normal to the faces; a positive pressure pushes onto the faces (FElupe: `−p J F⁻ᵀ dA`), a negative one pulls. A pressure is an item of the steps (`loads`, with the point loads; default: all), applied in full unless a step ramps it: a ramp item of a pressure is a factor (`p * ramp`), and it keeps its last value in the following steps. A pressure is not a curve of a job. The golden case `block_pressure` pulls the end face of a cube with symmetry planes; its reaction force equals the pressure times the deformed area of the end face. In the viewport, a pressure is drawn as arrows at the centers of the faces (at most 300), onto the faces or, for a pull, away from them.
- **Results of control points** (#101): after every substep, the runner records the displacement of every control point and the force of the constraints at it (the sum of `results.force` of the multi-point constraints: the reaction force of a boundary of the control point, or the force of a load at it); of a rotation point the rotation and the moment. The results show them in a card **Control points** (toolbar button **Points**) and in a tooltip on hover of a control point; **Export ▸ Control points (CSV)** writes all substeps (`substep, plate.u_x, …, plate.F_z`, of a rotation point `phi` and `M`; `felupe_studio.results.export(job, "csv")`).
- **Boundaries and load cases** (#82): a boundary draws a short stroke through every point of its point set along every fixed component (in the colors of the axis triad); a boundary that moves (a ramp of a step, with the end of the last step that ramps it, or a `value`) draws an arrow per point in the direction of its components and the sign of its value. Load cases and symmetry planes draw the boundaries of `fem.dof` (`app/src/viewer/boundaries.ts`, computed on the nodes of the drawn meshes; `tests/test_boundary_symbols.py` writes what FElupe makes of a set of load cases and symmetries into `boundaries.fixture.json`, which the app test compares). The eye of a boundary or load case hides its symbols, a selected one is highlighted. The size of the symbols follows the size of the model; more than 300 points of a boundary are thinned out. Point sets of point ids are not drawn.
- **Values per component** (#225): a boundary prescribes one `value` for all of its constrained components, or one per component in the order x, y, z: `fem.Boundary(..., skip=(0, 1, 0), value=[0.1, -0.2])`, so one boundary moves a point set diagonally instead of two boundaries on the same points. With two of three axes constrained the list has two values, the constrained components only (no placeholder). A ramp of such a boundary gives `start` and `end` one number per component; a number applies to all of them. The validation (shared cases) reports a list of another length than the constrained components, and a list in the ramp of a load case, a point load or a pressure, which have one value. In the form, *Value*, *Start* and *End* take numbers separated by commas (`0.5, -0.1`); one number stays a number (the schema type is a number or a list of 1 to 3 numbers, the `numbers` input with `collapse`). The viewport draws the arrow in the direction of the values, e.g. (5, −2) for y and z.
- **Nodes** (view toolbar, #91) shows the nodes of the drawn meshes as small black dots, in the Model and the Results workspace (one setting for both; off by default): the points of the surfaces of the cells that are not hidden, without the points inside the subdivided faces of quadratic cells (`isnode` of `felupe_studio.scene.surface()`); in the results, displaced like the surfaces.
- **Picking:** a click (not a drag) on a face highlights all drawn faces in its plane and previews its points. A face normal to x, y or z offers **Create point set**: the coordinate is the exact coordinate of the nodes in the plane (not of the float32 surface), with a larger `atol` only if their spread needs it, and the name is `x_min`, `x_max` or e.g. `y_0_5`. Other faces are explained; point sets select planes normal to an axis. Escape or × clears the pick. A tip in the viewport explains the click until the first pick (or until it is dismissed; remembered in the browser). The panels at the bottom (tip, picked face or nodes, selected point set, messages) are stacked between the axes and the job panel and never overlap; in a narrow view the job panel spans the width and the other overlays stay above it (#128).
- **Connected surfaces** (#189, `app/src/viewer/surfaces.ts`): a click on a face of the boundary of a section picks the faces that are reached from it over shared edges (two faces are neighbours if they share at least two nodes) while the normals of neighbouring faces differ by at most the feature angle, 30° by default (like VTK and ParaView: the edges of a box and chamfers of 45° stay apart, a cylinder of 12 or more segments is one surface); the panel offers 10° to 180°, kept in the browser (`felupe-studio.feature-angle`), and a changed angle follows the clicked faces. The panel describes the faces by the simplest selection: a plane normal to x, y or z (all normals along the axis, all nodes at one coordinate), a cylinder about an axis parallel to x, y or z (all normals normal to the axis, curved by more than 1°, the nodes on a circle of the least-squares fit of Kåsa within 1e-3 of the radius, with an `atol` that covers their spread), or a surface of nodes. *Create point set* stores the plane (or its bound, #181) or the cylinder only if it selects exactly the nodes of the surface on the drawn meshes, else the nodes; *Create face set* stores the plane only if it selects exactly the faces of the surface, else their centers; *Control point at center* takes the center of the points. Faces of meshes without a section pick their plane as before.
- **Combined surfaces** (#195, `app/src/viewer/patches.ts`), like `Mesh.select_surface_points()` of FElupe 12: the picked faces are a list of *patches*, each a clicked face (`seed`, its connected surface at the current angle, or a single face with Shift) of a section with a state, *selected*, *excluded* or *omitted* (a face of a selected patch removed with Shift+click). A click (or a tap) cycles the state of the clicked surface (#260): not picked → selected (a patch added to the others), selected → excluded (the selected patches of the face, and the faces omitted from them are dropped), excluded → removed. Shift+click (or a long press, #273) cycles a single face the same way; a face of a selected patch is omitted instead, and a second Shift+click takes it back. A right click (`pointerup` of the right button without a drag, which pans) is a shortcut: it removes the selected or excluded patches of the face at once (with Shift: the single face, or omits a face of a selected patch) and does nothing on a face that is not picked. The panel names what the next click on the last clicked face does (*click again to exclude*, *to deselect*, *to pick*). The faces are those of the selected patches without excluded or omitted faces; the points are their nodes without the nodes of excluded faces, so an edge shared with an excluded patch is not selected (excluded patches have priority). A changed angle recomputes the patches from their seeds with their states. Selected faces are highlighted, excluded faces dark red (`darkred`, like FElupe). The surface is described as above over all sections; *Create face set* needs faces of one section. Touch screens need no right click and no toggle (the toggle *Exclude* of #195 is gone). A click on the background keeps the patches; a click or a right click on a picked node deselects it. A cylinder also needs every face to span at most 60° about the axis, since the corners of a few planes (two sides of a box) are on a circle as well.
- **Long press** (#273, `app/src/viewer/viewer.ts`): a primary pointer held still for `LONG_PRESS` (500 ms) picks like Shift+click (`shift: true`), with a mouse, a pen and a touch screen alike; a ring (`.fv-press`) fades out at the pointer, and the click that follows is dropped. A move of more than `CLICK` (4 px, a rotation) or a second pointer (a pinch) cancels it. On a coarse pointer (`(pointer: coarse)`), the hints speak of taps and long presses: *Tap faces …*, *Long-press single faces*, *tap again to exclude*.
- **Picking nodes** (#93): with **Nodes** shown, a click within 8 pixels of a drawn node picks the node instead of the face (nodes behind the drawn faces are not picked); Shift+click (or a long press) adds or removes a node, a click on a picked node deselects it (#260), a click beside the nodes picks a face again and Escape or × clears them. The picked nodes are highlighted and stored with the exact coordinates of the nodes of the meshes (not the float32 coordinates of the surface). The panel shows the coordinates of a single node and offers **Copy** (one point per line), **Create point set** (by nodes, named `node` or `nodes`) and, if a point set by nodes is selected, **Add to** it.
- **Control points** (#95) are drawn as cyan dots with a dark outline, also without **Nodes**; the selected one is highlighted and its eye hides it. A click within 8 pixels selects a control point (before nodes and faces). The browser lists them in a group **Points**, the properties call them _Control point_. Boundaries of a control point draw their symbols at it. A control point at a node of the meshes is merged with that node by the script; the viewport says so for the selected one. In the results, the control points are displaced like the surfaces (`points` of the scene of `felupe_studio.results`: name, position and displacement).
- **Control point at center** (#96): the selected point set (with the number of its points and the center of their bounding box), a picked face and picked nodes offer a control point at the center of the bounding box of their points. It opens the dialog of a new control point with these coordinates (and a name like `control_right`), so the point can be moved off the face before it is created. The coordinates are stored, not the point set: the control point does not move when the point set changes.
- **Multi-point constraints** (#99) are drawn as thin cyan lines from the center point to the connected points (thinned out for more than 300 points, like the symbols of the boundaries); the selected one is highlighted and its eye hides it. For the selected constraint, the viewport reports points that a boundary or a load case prescribes in a connected component as well, e.g. `glue: 25 points are also in edge (z): over-constrained.` In the results, the lines follow the displaced points (the nearest node of the result surfaces, or the displacement of a control point).
- **Updates:** the build writes `version.json` with the build (the commit on GitHub Pages). An open app asks for it every ten minutes and when the page becomes visible again, without the HTTP cache, and offers **Reload** once a newer version is deployed; with a model, it asks to save the project first, because the project and its mesh files are not kept across a reload yet (#57).
- The toolbar fits the model into the view and sets the camera onto the xy-, xz- or yz-plane or back to the default view (*Iso*, key 1), in which the viewer opens: like the plots of FElupe, onto the xy-plane for a planar model. Drag to rotate, shift-drag (or right-drag) to pan, scroll to zoom, double-click to reset. The camera fits the model when it is first shown and keeps its rotation, zoom and pan while the model changes.
- The app talks to the viewer only through the adapter `app/src/viewer/viewer.ts`: meshes with colors of groups of cells and hidden cells, the legend, point markers, arrows and camera presets. The scenes are built by pure functions (`app/src/viewer/scene.ts`), tested without WebGL.

## Running jobs (app)

**Solve** (header) runs the jobs of the model in the Pyodide worker. It is offered for a valid model with a job and loaded mesh files, once Python is loaded; otherwise its tooltip says what is missing.

- `felupe_studio.runner.run()` runs the generated script (`codegen(..., evaluate=False, check_schema=False)`: the app validated the command list against the schema with Ajv, so the worker needs no `jsonschema`; the references are checked again) and evaluates every job with a plugin (`fem.Plugin`) that reports the progress: the substeps of every step at the start, then every substep, Newton iteration (norm of the residuals), failed attempt and converged substep. The worker posts these events to the main thread while Python runs. The results are the number of substeps and the time per job and, for a characteristic curve, the displacements and forces of its boundary per substep (three components each). The values of all fields after every converged substep are recorded for the results (see below).
- The panel in the viewport shows the job, step, substep and Newton iteration with a progress bar, in lines that do not wrap, so that it keeps its size while running (with a cutback, a line counts its retries); then the time per job and the force at the last substep of a curve. A failed run shows the exception and the full Python traceback; a failed Newton-Raphson method also suggests more substeps, the cutback or a looser tolerance.
- **Cutback** is a setting of the job (`cutback`, also in the generated script): `fem.CutbackPlugin` retries a substep that does not converge with smaller increments. Its parameters `factor`, `max_cutbacks` and `growth` are optional; without them, FElupe's defaults apply (0.5, 10 and 2.0), and only the given ones are written to the script. The panel counts these attempts.
- **Newton-Raphson method:** the tolerance of the job is always passed to `job.evaluate()`, `maxiter` only if it is given (FElupe's default: 16), both by `evaluate_options()` of `felupe_studio.codegen` for the script and the runner. The panel shows the iteration of the maximum, e.g. *Newton iteration 3 of 16*.
- **Linear solver** (#203): `solver` of a job chooses the solver of the linear systems of the Newton iterations, `job.evaluate(solver=...)`: `superlu` (`fem.solve.SuperLUSolver()`, the default of FElupe 12; without `solver` the script passes none), `spsolve` (`scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve`, the default of FElupe before v12) or `iterative` with a `method` of `scipy.sparse.linalg` (`minres`, `cg`, `gmres`, `bicgstab`), a relative tolerance `rtol` and an optional `maxiter`. An inexact linear solve makes the Newton iteration converge linearly with a rate of about `rtol`, so `rtol` is by default two orders of magnitude finer than the tolerance of the job (`solver_rtol()`: 1e-4 for 0.01, 1.49e-10 for 1.49e-8); a given `rtol` wins. `solver_source()` of `felupe_studio.codegen` writes the solver into the script (`solver=iterative_solver("minres", rtol=0.0001)`); the helpers `spsolve(A, b)` and `iterative_solver(method, rtol, maxiter)` are embedded only in scripts that use them. The runner evaluates the same source in the namespace of the script. `iterative_solver` checks `info` of SciPy and raises a `RuntimeError` if the method does not converge, so that the job fails with *The linear solver cg did not converge to rtol=0.0001 …* instead of continuing with a poor solution (with a cutback, the failed attempt is retried with a smaller increment). The schema allows `method`, `rtol` and `maxiter` only for `iterative`. The direct solvers have a `precision` (ADR 0016): `double` (the default) or `single`, which solves the linear equation system in `float32`: `solver=single_precision(fem.solve.SuperLUSolver())` casts the matrix and the right-hand side to `float32` and returns the increment in the precision of the right-hand side, so the model, the residual forces and the convergence check stay in double precision and the curves are those of double precision (within 2e-5 for the golden cases and the samples), with up to a quarter less memory and up to two times faster for large models (`docs/benchmarks.md`). The helper `single_precision(solver)` is embedded with the other helpers of the solvers. The schema forbids a `precision` of `iterative`. The browser and the panel of the running job show the solver, e.g. *minres, rtol 1e-4* or *SuperLU, single precision*. The golden case `bar_iterative_minres` (the bar of `bar_single_body` with `minres` and FElupe's tight tolerance 1.49e-8, so `rtol` = 1.49e-10: with a loose `rtol` the results would depend on the round-off of the machine) gives the curve of the direct solver within its tolerance of 0.01. The golden case `bar_two_materials_single_precision` is `bar_two_materials` with SuperLU in single precision, and every golden case with a direct solver is also solved in single precision against its curves (within the tolerance of the job, at most 1e-3 and at least 1e-6 of the largest force).
- **PARDISO** (#308): `solver.type` `pardiso` solves with PARDISO of Intel oneMKL, `solver=pypardiso.spsolve` (or `single_precision(pypardiso.spsolve)`), with `import pypardiso` only in scripts with such a job. It factorizes on several threads, so it is the direct solver for large 3D models in Python on the computer (`felupe-studio serve`, the extra `felupe-studio[pardiso]`); MKL has no build for WebAssembly, so not in Pyodide. Whether a runtime has it is not a property of the model: the model is valid everywhere, and `felupe_studio.capabilities` says what a runtime has. `capabilities()` gives the `runtime` (`pyodide` or `python`) and `pardiso` with the `version` of pypardiso and whether it factorizes in `single` precision (a solve of a 1×1 matrix in `float32`: PyPardiso 0.4 raises a `TypeError`, adtzlr/PyPardiso solves it), if it is installed. Both runtimes send it in their `ready` message. `capability_issues()` and `capabilityIssues()` in `app/src/core/capabilities.ts` give the issues of the jobs whose solver the runtime does not have: *PARDISO needs Python on your computer (felupe-studio serve)* in the browser, *PARDISO needs the package pypardiso* without it, and *PyPardiso … solves only in double precision* for a job in single precision (of its solver, or of a model in single precision, #297) without a PyPardiso in `float32`. The app shows them after the errors of the model, like the MCP server (`issues()` and `solve`), and `run()` of the runner raises them before a script runs. *Help ▸ About* of the local runtime shows the version of PyPardiso and its precisions. The golden case `bar_pardiso` is `bar_two_materials` with PARDISO: its script is checked everywhere, its results only where pypardiso is installed (and in single precision with a PyPardiso in `float32`), and its stored curve is that of SuperLU within 1e-10. `benchmark(n, solver="pardiso")` of `felupe_studio.benchmark` times it in CPython (`docs/benchmarks.md`).
- **Tolerance** is a setting of the job (`tol`, default 0.01, `TOL` of `felupe_studio.codegen`, #176): the tolerance of the Newton-Raphson method, checked by FElupe (since 12.1) as `norm(r[active]) < atol + tol * f_ref`: the norm of the residual forces relative to a reference force `f_ref`, the larger of the norm of the reaction forces and the largest norm of the forces that the other items exert on an item (e.g. a pressure or a contact on a solid body, so the criterion stays relative for forces that are not reacted at the boundaries), with an absolute tolerance `atol` at the round-off errors of the residual forces. The default is looser than FElupe's (the square root of the machine epsilon, 1.49e-8), like the force criterion of commercial codes (Abaqus and ANSYS check 0.5 % of a reference force): in the golden cases it halves the time, and the forces deviate by less than 1 % of the largest force (`test_default_tolerance`). A job without `tol` means 0.01; projects of v0.3 and older are not migrated, so their results change slightly. Golden cases that are compared with converged references (the material cases `cube_<model>` and `bar_two_materials`) set `tol: 1.49e-8`.
- **Live results** (#207): while Python runs, the worker cannot answer requests (e.g. `result` for a scene), so the runner pushes the live results: the plugin `felupe_studio.runner.Live`, after the `Recorder`, reports an event `live` after every converged substep with the `substep` (counted from 1 over all steps) and, of a characteristic curve, the point `x`, `y` of its curve (the same values as the final result). With the settings `live = {quantity, interval?, budget?}` of `run()` (the message `run` of the worker gets them from the app: the quantity of the Results view when Solve was pressed, unless *Live results* is off), the event also has the `scene` of the current fields (`results.current_scene()`, like `scene()` without restoring a substep) when it is due: at the last substep of every step, else at most every `interval` seconds (default 1) and only while the time spent on scenes is at most `budget` (default 0.1) of the time of the job. `run_json()` gives events with a scene to `report_scene(event, meta, surfaces)`, and the worker posts them as `job-progress` with the bytes of the surfaces transferred. `JobRunner` keeps the live results of every job of a run (`advanceLive()`: the last converged substep, the curve points, the last scene and its substep) in the run state, also of a failed or cancelled run. `liveResults()` (`app/src/core/live.ts`) makes the shown job (chosen, else the running one, else the last one) a finished-like run for the controls and the curve card, with its status (*Live · substep 7 of 20*, *job_1 finished*, *Failed/Cancelled after substep 7 of 20*). `ResultsView` shows the live scene instead of loading scenes (the range of the contour is that of the scene, unless fixed), keeps the camera, and shows the status; the substep slider and the player are disabled, and a quantity chosen during the run is kept for the results of the run and the next one (*Live: … · changes apply after the run*). The choices of the view (quantity, scale, curve axes) are kept across runs. *Live results* in the Results ribbon (`felupe-studio.live`) turns the scenes off; the curve points are always sent.
- **Cancel:** Python runs synchronously in the worker, so a run is cancelled by terminating the worker. A new worker loads Python again (Pyodide and the wheels come from the cache) and gets the mesh files again (`PyodideClient.restart()`).
- **tensortrax** (the models of `fem.Hyperelastic`, e.g. Mooney-Rivlin or Ogden) is a pinned wheel that is loaded only before the first run of a model with one of its materials (`lazy` in `config.wheels`), so it does not add to the start-up time. FElupe was imported before, so `load_tensortrax()` reloads `felupe.constitution` to make `fem.Hyperelastic` available.

## Results (app)

**Show results** (job panel) or the **Results** workspace shows the last finished run: the deformed sections with a contour, like `fem.ViewSolid(field, solid).plot(...)` with the plotting backend `"webgl"` of FElupe.

- The ribbon chooses the job (with more than one), the contour, the substep (counted over all steps of the job, the last one first), the scale of the displacements and the transparent undeformed shape. For a characteristic curve, it shows the displacement and the force of the curve boundary at the substep.
- Contours: the displacement (magnitude or a component) at the points; the principal values (max., mid., min.) of the logarithmic strain and of the Cauchy stress and the von Mises equivalent of the Cauchy stress as the mean of the quadrature points of every cell, or at the points (#231, below). By default, the range of the colors is the range of all cells of all sections at this substep (one scalar bar).
- **Values at the points** (#231, *Values at* in the contour settings, `method` of `scene()`, `ranges()`, `export()` and of the live scenes; `felupe_studio.results.METHODS`): `cells` (the default), `mean` (the means of the cells averaged at the points, `fem.topoints(..., mean=True)`), `project` (`fem.project`, an L2 projection) or `topoints` (`fem.topoints`). Per section, with the region of its displacement field: the values are discontinuous between sections. At the points, the strain and the stress tensors are moved (their six independent components, `UPPER`) and the invariants are evaluated there (#274, `_point_data()`): the von Mises stress is never negative, and the principal values are those of the tensor at a point. Before, the invariants of the quadrature points were moved, and their projection overshot and undershot, e.g. a von Mises stress of -0.27 MPa beside the air of the engine mount. The equivalent plastic strain, a state variable, is moved itself and clipped at zero. At the cells nothing changed: the means of the invariants of the quadrature points. The tensors cost six columns of the projection instead of one or three: a scene of the engine mount takes about 70 ms instead of 30 ms (CPython). The projection solves its mass matrix by the conjugate gradient method with a Jacobi preconditioner (about ten times faster than the sparse direct solver of `fem.project` for 30 000 points). Quadratic triangles and tetrahedrons and the MINI elements are moved to their corner points, with the linear cells and the same quadrature points (the quadrature of quadratic triangles and tetrahedrons is too low for a projection with their own shape functions); the other points of quadratic cells get the linear interpolation of their corners. `topoints` needs cells whose quadrature points are at their points (Gauss-Legendre with as many quadrature points as points: linear and tri-quadratic hexahedrons, linear and bi-quadratic quads); with another section the values are projected instead (`method_of()`), the scene has the `method` of its values and whether they can be shifted (`shift`), and the app shows *Points: shifted* disabled.
- `felupe_studio.results`: a plugin records copies of the values of all fields of the top-level field container after every substep. `scene(job, substep, quantity)` writes the values of a substep back into the fields in-place (the fields of the sections share them, see `fem.field.merge`) and evaluates the quantity per section. The pressure and the volume ratio of a `fem.SolidBodyNearlyIncompressible` are a state of the solid body, which an evaluation updates by a linearization at the previous one: the stresses of a substep are evaluated twice, so that they do not depend on the substep shown before (#226). The outer surface of every section is triangulated once per run (`triangulate_surface`, subdivided for quadratic cells; the bubble points of the MINI element are left out). The arrays go to the main thread as bytes, like the surfaces of the meshes; the viewer adds the scaled displacements to the points.
- **Contour settings** (#139, the button beside *Contour*, kept in the browser): the color map (Turbo, Viridis, Cool–warm, Grayscale; `app/src/core/colormaps.ts`), continuous or 6–20 bands, and the range: of the substep, over all substeps of the job (`felupe_studio.results.ranges(job, quantity)`: the range of every substep, kept until the next run) or manual. An automatic range of the diverging Cool–warm map is centered at zero for signed values. Values outside of a manual range are drawn in an extra color: the lookup table of the surfaces (`lut` of the viewer) gets the extra color as its first and last entry (or band), and the range of the surfaces is extended by one entry (or band), so the values within the range keep their colors. The app draws the scalar bar itself (`app/src/viewer/scalarbar.ts`), with the classes of the scalar bars of the viewer inside its root, so the picture export takes it: the map over the range, a swatch of the extra color below or above it, and ticks at the limits of the bands.
- **Minimum and maximum** (#140, *Min/Max* in the results toolbar): `scene()` returns the `extremes` of the values of all sections, each with its value, section, `at` (`"point"` or `"cell"`), index (the node of the merged mesh or the cell of the section), whether it is at the surface, and its position and displacement (of the node, or the mean of the points of the cell). The view marks a node with a dot and a cell at the surface with the outlines of its outer faces (the triangles of the scene with the cell as `owners`, grouped by `faces`; `app/src/viewer/extremes.ts`); a cell inside gets a dot at its center. The labels are buttons in the root of the viewer (`app/src/viewer/labels.ts`), moved with the camera once per frame; a label of a cell takes the center of one of its outer faces: once the camera rests, the nearest one to the camera that is not behind drawn faces (a ray test on the drawn surfaces), otherwise the label is dimmed. A click on a label or a marker shows where the value is, a hover the tooltip of the value; the picture export draws the labels.
- Only one scene is loaded at a time: while the slider of the substeps moves, the latest substep is loaded next. Loaded scenes are kept for the run (#141, `app/src/core/scenecache.ts`), the least recently used ones are dropped above 128 MB of arrays.
- **Playing the substeps** (#141, ▷ beside the slider or Space, `app/src/ui/usePlayer.ts`): the next substep (after the last one the first, or with *Back and forth* (⇄ beside ▷) the one before it, turning at the first and the last substep; `advance` of `app/src/core/results.ts`) is shown once the scene of the current one is shown and its time is over (1–20 substeps per second), so a slow scene delays the play instead of skipping substeps. While playing, the scenes of the next four substeps in the order of playing are loaded after the wanted one. A scene that fails stops playing; playing stops outside of the Results workspace and while solving. Scenes are loaded only while the Results workspace is shown. A new run replaces the results (also if it fails or is cancelled); results stay available after a change of the model, with a note.
- **Value under the pointer** (#85): hovering over the deformed sections shows a tooltip with the quantity of the contour and its value: for cell data the value of the cell of the hit face (_Cell 42 of block_, the index of the cell in its section), for point data the value of the nearest corner of the hit face with its three displacements (_Node 17 of block_, the index of the point in the merged mesh; a point inside a subdivided quadratic face has no node), marked with a ring. The hit test runs on the drawn (deformed and scaled) surfaces once per frame (`app/src/viewer/probe.ts`); the result scene has the cell of every triangle (`owners`) and the node of every point (`nodes`). Rotating, panning or zooming hides the tooltip.
- The recorded fields take memory in the worker: the values of all fields (float64) per substep.
- **State variables** (#191): the plugin records the state variables of the solid bodies whose material has state variables of its own (`fem.MaterialStrain` with `nstatevars > 0`, the elastic-plastic model) after every substep, and `scene()` restores them with the fields. Then the stress of a substep is the stress of its state (no strain increment), not the one of a return mapping from another substep. A linear-elastic `fem.MaterialStrain` does not need them (its stress is linear in the strain increment). They take 28 numbers (float64) per quadrature point and substep: e.g. 9 MB per substep for 5 000 hexahedrons. The contour `plastic_strain` (*Equivalent plastic strain*, offered with an elastic-plastic material) is the first state variable, zero in sections of other materials; the VTU and XDMF exports add it as `Equivalent Plastic Strain`.
- **Force-displacement curve** (a job with a curve boundary, `fem.CharacteristicCurve`): a card over the results shows the reaction force over the displacement of the curve boundary per substep, by default of the component with the largest displacement at the end. The current substep is marked; hovering shows the values of the nearest substep (by the horizontal axis if its values are monotonic, else by the distance in the plane), a click selects it. **Table** lists the values of the axes (a click on a row selects the substep), **CSV** downloads all components with the substeps and steps. **Curve** in the ribbon shows or hides the card.
- **Axes of the curve** (#197, `app/src/core/curve.ts`): *x* and *y* in the card choose the quantity of each axis among the components of the displacement and of the force of the curve boundary (`ux` … `Fz`, of a rotation point `φx` … `Mz`) and the substep, so e.g. uy over ux, Fz over the substep or Fz over Fz. The runner records all three components per substep, so no other results are needed. The title follows the axes (*Force–displacement* by default, e.g. *Force–substep*), and the results panel shows the values of the chosen axes at the substep. **Axes** opens a popover with the options of each axis: a linear or a log scale (the logarithm of the absolute values; zeros are left out, with a note in the card; the ticks are powers of ten), **Negate** (e.g. a compression as positive values; the labels and the table show −F), and a **fixed range** (both limits, positive on a log scale; it starts with the shown range, the curve is cut off outside). The options are kept in the browser (`felupe-studio.curve`), like the settings of the contour; the quantities of the axes are kept with the view of the results.

## Session in the browser (app)

The app keeps the current project in the browser, so that a reload (or a crash of the tab) restores it: the project file (text), its file name, whether it has unsaved changes, and the mesh files that its `mesh.import` commands read. The undo history and the results are not kept; solve again after a reload.

- `app/src/core/session.ts`: IndexedDB database `felupe-studio` with the stores `session` (one record: project, file name, modified, names, sizes and SHA-256 hashes of the files) and `files` (the content by file name). A file whose hash did not change is not written again; files that the project does not import any more are removed. Only the last session is kept, also with several tabs (the last change wins).
- The session is written 0.4 s after the last change. If the mesh files do not fit into the storage of the browser, the project is kept without them; a storage that does not work does not break the app. The status bar says where the session is kept (_Kept in this browser_, _… without mesh files_, _Not kept in this browser_, with the reason as tooltip).
- On start, the last session is restored if the model is still empty; the mesh files are written into the worker and inspected again.
- **File ▸ New project** starts an empty project and clears the kept session; with unsaved changes it asks first. The data stays in this browser only: save the project (file or archive) to keep it elsewhere. The file name of the session also keeps the format of the project (#150).

## Local Python runtime

`felupe-studio serve` (#240, ADR 0014, `pip install "felupe-studio[serve]"`) serves the built app on `127.0.0.1` (port 8765, `--port`; `--no-browser`; `--app <dist>`, default the app of the package or `app/dist` of a clone) and runs the Python side with CPython. It prints the URL with a token and opens it.

- `felupe_studio.serve` is a Starlette app: `/` and `/index.html` with `<meta name="felupe-studio-runtime" content="python" />`, the files of the app, and the WebSocket `/runtime`. Only the hosts `127.0.0.1` and `localhost` are accepted (`TrustedHostMiddleware`, against DNS rebinding); the WebSocket needs `?token=` and, if it has an `Origin`, the own origin (else it is closed before it is accepted: HTTP 403). uvicorn takes frames up to 2 GiB, uncompressed.
- Every connection starts a process (`multiprocessing`, spawn) with a temporary folder: `felupe_studio.runtime.session()` imports FElupe, sends `ready` with the versions (`runtime: "python"`, `felupe_studio`) and answers the requests with `handle()` of `felupe_studio.requests`. A closed connection ends the process, also a running job, and removes its folder.
- **One request layer for both runtimes** (#311): `felupe_studio.requests.handle(request, send)` answers the requests of `app/src/worker/protocol.ts` (without `init`) with the messages of the worker: `write-file`, `inspect-mesh`, `make-mesh`, `run` (with its progress and live scenes), `script`, `script-view`, `check` (local only: Pyodide has no `jsonschema`), `result`, `result-ranges`, `export-results`, `export-mesh` and `benchmark` (`HANDLERS`). The arrays of surfaces and scenes and the exported files are bytes; an error is an `error` with the traceback. The local runtime calls `handle()` in its process. The Pyodide worker calls `handle_frame(frame, send)` with the frame of the request (`encodeFrame()`) and gets every answer as `send(text, buffers)`, the parts of a frame (`felupe_studio.frames.split()`: the JSON text with `{"$bytes": i}` and the buffers), which it restores (`restore()` of `frames.ts`), turns into typed arrays (`toWorkerMessage()` of `messages.ts`, like `LocalChannel`) and posts with the arrays transferred. So both runtimes get the same messages, JSON included (e.g. `null`), and the bytes are copied once. The TypeScript worker keeps what is its own: loading Pyodide and the wheels, the bytecode cache (ADR 0017), the lazy tensortrax wheel before a `run`, and the transfer. `tests/test_requests.py` runs the requests on both paths in CPython.
- Frames (`felupe_studio.frames`: `encode()`, `decode()`): a message without bytes is JSON text; with bytes a binary frame: the length of the header (4 bytes, little-endian), the header `{"message": …, "buffers": [[offset, length], …]}` with `{"$bytes": i}` in place of the i-th buffer, padded with spaces, then the buffers, each padded to 8 bytes.
- The Pyodide worker leaves `runtime.py`, `serve.py`, `cli.py` and `__main__.py` out, like `mcp.py`; it has `requests.py` and `frames.py`.
- The package: `hatch_build.py` puts `app/dist` into the wheel as `felupe_studio/data/app`, without `wheels/` (Pyodide) and `samples/`: the wheel has the folders of the samples of the build in `felupe_studio/data/samples` (#350; without a built app those of `app/samples`), which the server serves at `/samples` with their list `/samples/index.json`, made from the folder when it starts (`read_samples()`; a folder that breaks the rules stops `felupe-studio serve` with the message); the sdist has the built app too, for its wheel. Without a built app the wheel has no app, unless `FELUPE_STUDIO_REQUIRE_APP` is set (CI and the release build the app first). `tests/check_wheel.py` starts `felupe-studio serve` of the installed wheel and connects to it; the CI job *Local runtime* runs `pytest -m e2e` (`tests/e2e`, Playwright): a sample opened from `felupe-studio serve`, solved with CPython, its results shown, and a reload.
- The app (`app/src/worker/local.ts`): with the meta tag, `createClient()` makes the `RuntimeClient` with a `LocalChannel` instead of the worker: a WebSocket with the interface of the worker (`postMessage`, `addEventListener`, `terminate`). It leaves out `init`, encodes and decodes the frames (`frames.ts`, shared files `tests/data/frames/python.bin` and `app.bin`, each side decodes the other's) and turns the bytes into the typed arrays of the worker (`toWorkerMessage()` of `messages.ts`, like the Pyodide worker). A restart (*Cancel*) closes the connection and opens a new one; the client writes its files again. A closed connection is an error without an id: the start or the pending requests fail, and the runtime is not ready any more (*The local Python runtime is not reachable …*). The token comes from `?token=`, is kept in the `sessionStorage` of the tab (for a reload) and removed from the address. The local app registers no service worker; the status bar says *FElupe … ready, local Python …* (and the version of the package if it differs from the app), *About* lists the runtime and its versions, the welcome page says that Python runs on this computer, and opening a project with mesh scripts shows them first, also without parameters: they run on this computer with the rights of the user.

## Agent bridge

`felupe-studio serve --agent` (#258, ADR 0015, `pip install "felupe-studio[serve,mcp]"`) also offers an MCP endpoint for AI agents at `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/mcp` (Streamable HTTP of the SDK) and prints how to add it, e.g. `claude mcp add --transport http felupe-studio http://127.0.0.1:8765/mcp --header "Authorization: Bearer <token>"`. The token is new at every start, or the one of the environment variable `FELUPE_STUDIO_TOKEN` (`token_of()`, at least 16 letters, digits, `_` or `-`), which a `.mcp.json` takes as `"Authorization": "Bearer ${FELUPE_STUDIO_TOKEN}"`. Its tools act on the model of one tab of the app.

- `/mcp` (`TokenGuard`): the token as `Authorization: Bearer` (else 401), no `Origin` header (no website, else 403), the hosts of the loopback interface (`TrustedHostMiddleware` and the DNS rebinding protection of the SDK). The served `index.html` has `<meta name="felupe-studio-agent" content="on" />`.
- `/agent`: the WebSocket of the tab, with `?token=` and the own origin like `/runtime`. The tab starts with `{"type": "hello", "version": 1}`; the server answers `{"type": "welcome", "version": 1}`, or `refused` (another version) and closes. A newly connected tab replaces the former one, which gets `{"type": "replaced"}`; its pending calls fail.
- `felupe_studio.bridge.Bridge` forwards a call as `{"type": "call", "id", "tool", "arguments"}` (without unset arguments) and waits for `{"type": "result", "id", "result"}` or `{"type": "error", "id", "message"}` (a failed tool for the agent); `{"type": "progress", "id", "progress", "total", "message"}` becomes an MCP progress notification. The calls run one after the other. Without a tab a tool fails: *No app is connected. Open the app of felupe-studio serve in a browser and choose Help ▸ Connect an agent.*
- The tools (`agent_server()`): `schema` (answered by the server), `samples`, `open_sample`, `list_commands`, `add_command`, `update_command`, `delete_command`, `parameters`, `set_parameters`, `write_file`, `check`, `script`, `solve` (with progress), `results`, `selection`, `select`, `show` and `picture` (the tab answers `{"png": <base64>}`, the agent gets an image). The tools that the MCP server of project folders has too take their descriptions from `felupe_studio.mcp.DESCRIPTIONS`.
- `bridge.py` is left out of the Pyodide worker, like `mcp.py`.
- The app (`app/src/agent/`): with the meta tag (and the local runtime), *Help ▸ Connect an agent* opens an `AgentConnection` (`connection.ts`) to `/agent` with the token of the tab: it says hello, follows `welcome`, `refused` and `replaced`, and answers the calls one after the other with `agentTools()` (`tools.ts`), sending `result`, `error` or `progress`. The tools work on an `AgentHost`, the app as `useAppAgent()` and `useAgent()` (`app/src/ui/useAgent.ts`) give it at every call: the store, the files, the runtime, the job runner, the notes, the selection, the view and its picture. A change is checked against the schema (`commandErrors`, like the command line) and the names, and is one `store.apply`/`store.update`; the answers have `errors` (the issues) and `notes` of the app. `write_file` of a `.py` waits for the user (`ScriptConsent`: *Show the script*, *Run it*, *Do not run it*); a rejected script is not added. `check` asks the local runtime (`{"type": "check"}`, `felupe_studio.checks`, not in Pyodide) and shows the report in the browser. `solve` runs the app's Solve and reports the converged substeps of all jobs. The status bar shows the connection with *Disconnect*.
- Shared cases: `tests/data/tool_cases.json` (`python tests/data/make_tool_cases.py`) has sequences of `add_command`, `update_command` and `delete_command` from `two_sections.json` with the answers of `felupe_studio.mcp.Session`: the errors and notes, the changed command and its index, the names of all commands, or the message of a refusal (of a refusal by the schema only its prefix, the validators word it differently). The tools of the app give the same answers (`toolcases.test.ts`). Both refuse a second object of a name (*Not added: there is already an object 'right'.*).
- End to end (`tests/e2e/test_agent_bridge.py`, `pytest -m e2e`, the CI job *Local runtime and agent bridge*): `serve --agent`, a tab of Playwright connected with *Help ▸ Connect an agent*, the MCP client of the SDK over HTTP: a sample opened, a mesh script written and run by the user, a parametric mesh of it added and deleted, solved with progress, the results, the view of the results and its picture.

## Samples (app)

**File ▸ Samples…** and the welcome panel of an empty model (#127) list finished models with a picture; **Open** loads one like a project file with its mesh files, as a project archive `<id>.felupe.zip` (#150; a sample replaces the current model; the dialog warns about unsaved changes).

- The samples are folders `app/samples/NN_<id>` (#350) with a project file `model.felupe.json`, its mesh files, its description `sample.json` (title, description, topics) and a picture `thumbnail.svg`: the outer faces of the sections in the colors and the default view of the viewport, made from the model that the generated script builds (without evaluating the jobs). All of them are made by `app/scripts/make_samples.py` (units: mm, N, MPa); run it from the repository root after a change and commit the files.
- The brand of a build (ADR 0020, #321): `BRAND_DIR` or the default brand `app/brand/`. `brandOptions()` of `app/src/vite/brand.ts` reads and checks its `brand.json` (`brand.schema.json`, Ajv) in the config of Vite, before the plugins, and gives the folder of the samples: of `SAMPLES_DIR`, else of the brand (`samples`, relative to the brand folder), else none; `SAMPLES_DIR` beside `BRAND_DIR` is an error. `readBrand()` checks the files too: the logo must be an SVG, the icons PNGs of their size (their header, `pngSize()`). The plugin `brand()` copies the logo and the icons to `brand/` of the build (`brandFiles()`), writes `manifest.webmanifest` (`manifest()`: the names, the logo with `sizes: "any"`, the icons, `display: standalone` and the colors of the bar and the background of the app) and serves both in dev; it writes the title of `index.html`, the links of the favicon (the logo), the icon for the home screen and the manifest, the theme color, the accent color (the variables of the theme in a `<style>`, with the text color on the accent by its luminance) and the brand as a JSON block into the pages, without what only the build reads and with the URL of the logo (`pageBrand()`), after Vite (`order: "post"`), so the links are files of the build and not of the source; `define` gives it as `__BRAND__` too. In the app, `currentBrand()` of `app/src/brand.ts` reads the block of the page, else `__BRAND__` (the tests in Node), and `app.ts` takes `BRAND`, `APP_NAME` and the `LINKS` from it; the upstream repository, its license and FElupe stay constants. The messages of a project of a newer version name the app by `appName()`. The browser storage takes the prefix of the brand (`storage`): `storageKey(name)` gives the keys of `localStorage` and `sessionStorage` (the contour, the curve, the live results, the picking, the token of `serve`), `IndexedDBSession` the name of the session, `bytecodeCache()` the folder of the bytecode cache, which the client sends to the worker in the message `init` (`/pycache` for `felupe-studio`), and `workerUrl()` registers `sw.js?storage=<prefix>`, whose cache is `<prefix>-runtime-v1` and which removes only the older caches of its own prefix; FElupe Studio registers `sw.js` without it. A new `model.settings` takes `defaults.units` of the brand (`initial` of the registry).
- A build takes one folder of samples (#350): `app/samples`, or another one with `SAMPLES_DIR=~/my-samples npm run build` (or `npm run dev`, see `CONTRIBUTING.md`). The plugin `app/src/vite/samples.ts` reads it with the rules of `felupe_studio.samples` (the order of the prefixes of the folders, the ids without them, `sample.json`, the files of the project), copies their files to `samples/<folder>/` of the build and writes their list `samples/index.json`, and serves both in dev. The app loads the list when it starts (`loadSamples()`, the hook `useSamples()` of `App`, which passes it to the welcome panel, the dialog, the menu *File ▸ Samples…* and the tools of an agent): no list of samples is compiled into the app, and without a list the app has no samples. It loads the files of a sample from its folder (`sampleUrl()`); a sample without a picture shows the placeholder. `EXTRA_SAMPLES_DIR` (#255) and `BUILT_IN_SAMPLES` (#279) are replaced by it.
- `tests/test_samples.py` checks that every sample is a canonical project file without errors, that its files exist and its picture is up to date, and solves it with FElupe. The material test is checked against the analytical curve of the incompressible Neo-Hooke model. The hyperelastic block with a rigid plate (#102) shows control points, a multi-point constraint with a rotation point and a ramp with its own values per item; its test checks that the plate is moved by 8 mm and tilted by 0.1 rad. The 2D samples (#175) are an axisymmetric sandwich spring (the section of the discs and the sheets, pressed along the axis; its test checks that the stiffness is inversely proportional to the number of layers) and a quarter of a plate with a hole in plane strain with two symmetry planes (its test checks the concentration of the stress at the hole). Their pictures show the cells in the xy-plane. The hyperelastic spring with end stops (#222) is the example ex06 of FElupe (its mesh with flipped cells, whose volumes were negative): its test runs ex06 on the same mesh and compares the forces of the lower plate in z and x within 1e-3. The metacone (#241) is the example ex15 of FElupe, axisymmetric: its mesh script makes the mesh of ex15 with the default parameters, and its test runs ex15 and compares the force within 1e-8 (with FElupe's tolerance). The elastic-plastic plate with a hole (#269) is the plate in steel (strain-based, isotropic hardening), pulled by 0.1 mm and back: its test checks that the first substep has the stiffness of the same plate of the linear elastic material of small strains, that the unloading has it too and crosses zero force at a remaining elongation, and that the equivalent plastic strain is largest at the top of the hole. The bushing made with mesh tools (#314) is the bushing of the script with its meshes made by the mesh tools (lines, `embed`, `fill_between`, `add_runouts`, `revolve`, and the sleeves as revolved rectangles, two meshes of steel instead of one stacked mesh): its test checks that the soft body is the mesh of the script, the sleeves have its points, and the curve is that of the sample of the script within 1e-6. The engine mount (#241) is the example ex07 of FElupe, in plane strain, on its meshes (`app/scripts/ex07`, the cells of the metal tagged as the inner and the outer part by the radius of their centers): its test runs the vertical step of ex07 and compares the vertical force within 1e-8 (with FElupe's tolerance).

## Script view (app)

The ribbon tab **Script** of the Model workspace shows the script of `felupe_studio.runner.script_view()` instead of the viewport: the exported script and the lines of its objects (`felupe_studio.codegen.object_lines()`, from the syntax tree of the script). An object owns its assignment and the parts of other statements about it: an entry of the boundaries, the region, field and solid body of a section, the ramps of a step, the evaluation of a job; lines about several objects (e.g. the merge of the fields) belong to none. The view is made again 0.25 s after a change (the latest request wins), highlights Python (`app/src/core/highlight.ts`), highlights the lines of the selected object and selects the object of a clicked line. A model with issues shows the issues instead.

## Exports (app)

**Export** (header, and the section *Export* of the File menu) lists the exports of the project; an export that is not possible says why. **Help** links the user guide and the command reference, lists the keyboard shortcuts and shows *About* (the versions of the app, FElupe, Python, Pyodide and the wheels, the load time, the license and the Pyodide benchmark).

- **Python script** (`<project>.py`): the FElupe script of the model, generated in the worker (`felupe_studio.runner.script()`, without the schema check, see _Running jobs_). It reads the mesh files from its folder, so it runs next to them with FElupe, meshio and NumPy (and tensortrax for the models of `fem.Hyperelastic`).
- **Results of the job (XDMF)** (`<project>-<job>.xdmf`) and **Results of the substep (VTU)** (`<project>-<job>-<substep>.vtu`, the substep of the Results workspace), for ParaView: `felupe_studio.results.export()` writes the merged points that the cells use (without the bubble points of the MINI element) and one block of cells per section with meshio. Point data: `Displacement`; cell data (means of the quadrature points, the names of `fem.ViewSolid`): `Principal Values of Logarithmic Strain`, `Principal Values of Cauchy Stress`, `Equivalent of Cauchy Stress` and the index of the `Section`. With values at the points (#231), the strains and the stresses are point data too, with the same names: the means of the sections at their common points. The XDMF file is a time series with the substeps as time and the data inside the XML (`data_format="XML"`), so it is a single file without HDF5 (and h5py is not needed in the worker).
- **A mesh** (`<project>-<mesh>.<ext>`, from the menu of a mesh in the browser, #230): `felupe_studio.meshes.export()` reads the mesh file again (or runs the script with the parameters of the model and takes its item) and writes the blocks of solid cells of the types of FElupe's regions (one type with `cell_type`) with their integer cell data (the tags), the points in 3D (z = 0 of a 2D mesh) and multiplied by `scale`. The writers of meshio by extension (`EXPORT_FORMATS`): `vtu`, `vtk`, `msh` (Gmsh 4.1), `inp` (Abaqus) and `bdf` (Nastran). No XDMF: meshio's writer of single meshes needs h5py, which Pyodide lacks (the results use its time series writer with XML data). A test reads every format back.
- **Picture of the view** (`<project>.png`, or `<project>-<job>-<substep>.png` in the Results workspace): the 3D view at the resolution of the screen, with the scalar bar, the legend and the axis triad on the background of the viewport. The vendored viewer does not keep its drawing buffer, so the canvas is copied in the animation frame after its next draw (`app/src/viewer/snapshot.ts`) and the overlays are drawn on top.
